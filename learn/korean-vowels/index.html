Korean Vowels: From ㅏ to ㅢ

2026-11-07 · 4 min read · ggook's studio

Three strokes make every vowel

Every Korean vowel is built from three simple ideas from the original 1446 design: a dot for heaven, a flat line for the earth (ㅡ) and a standing line for a person (ㅣ). The dot is now written as a short stroke. Put the short stroke on the right of the standing line and you get ㅏ; put it on top of the flat line and you get ㅗ.

The 10 basic vowels

Vowels are written with a silent ㅇ when they appear on their own, so the examples below are whole syllables. Tap to listen.

One extra stroke adds a "y"

Look at the pairs ㅏ → ㅑ, ㅓ → ㅕ, ㅗ → ㅛ and ㅜ → ㅠ. Doubling the short stroke adds a y sound in front of the vowel. Learn four vowels and you get four more for free.

The two tricky ones: ㅓ and ㅡ

English speakers usually find ㅓ and ㅡ the hardest. For ㅓ, open your mouth as for "aw" but don't round your lips: it's an open, relaxed sound, different from the rounded ㅗ. For ㅡ, say "oo", then spread your lips into a flat smile while keeping your tongue where it is. Compare 어 and 오, then 우 and 으, a few times each.

Tall and flat vowels

The shape of a vowel decides where it goes in a syllable block. Tall vowels (ㅏ ㅑ ㅓ ㅕ ㅣ) sit to the right of the consonant, as in 나. Flat vowels (ㅗ ㅛ ㅜ ㅠ ㅡ) sit underneath, as in 노.

The 11 compound vowels

Compound vowels are two basic vowels joined into one letter. You don't have to learn them as new shapes: look at the parts.

Notice the pattern: ㅗ only joins with ㅏ, ㅐ or ㅣ, and ㅜ only joins with ㅓ, ㅔ or ㅣ. Vowels whose short stroke points up or right go together, and so do vowels whose stroke points down or left. You'll never see ㅗ joined with ㅓ.

Spelled differently, sounding the same

Hangul spelling reflects how Korean sounded when it was standardized, and a few vowels have since merged in everyday speech. This is good news: there's less to hear than there is to read.

  • ㅐ and ㅔ sound the same for most speakers today, like the e in "bed". 개 (dog) and 게 (crab) are told apart by context, and you have to learn the spelling of each word.
  • ㅙ, ㅞ and ㅚ are usually all pronounced "we". The official pronunciation standard allows ㅚ to be said as "we", so 회사 often sounds like "hwesa".
  • ㅢ changes with its position. At the start of a word it's "ui", as in 의자. After a consonant it's pronounced ㅣ: 희망 (hope) sounds like 히망. When 의 means "of" (as in 나의, "my"), it's usually pronounced 에.
