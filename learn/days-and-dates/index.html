Days, Weeks and Dates in Korean

2026-11-07 · 4 min read · ggook's studio

Talking about time

Making plans, reading a schedule or answering "when?" all need the same small set of words. This guide covers the days of the week, words like "yesterday" and "next week", parts of the day and how to ask for and say the date. For the numbers inside dates, see our guide to Korean numbers.

The days of the week

Every Korean day name ends in 요일 ("day of the week"). The first syllable is the part to learn, and each one has a meaning: the sun, the moon and the five elements of traditional East Asian thought, which were also linked to the five planets visible to the naked eye.

DayKoreanFirst syllable means
Monday월요일moon
Tuesday화요일fire
Wednesday수요일water
Thursday목요일wood
Friday금요일metal, gold
Saturday토요일earth
Sunday일요일sun

A memory trick: the week starts with the moon, runs through fire, water, wood, metal and earth, and ends with the sun. On calendars, timetables and opening hours, the days are often shortened to the first syllable: 월, 화, 수, 목, 금, 토, 일.

The weekend is 주말 ("end of the week") and a weekday is 평일. On Fridays you'll often hear 주말 잘 보내세요: have a good weekend.

Yesterday, today and tomorrow

Korean has single words for the days around today, including two that English needs a phrase for:

KoreanMeaning
그저께the day before yesterday
어제yesterday
오늘today
내일tomorrow
모레the day after tomorrow

You'll also hear the shorter 그제 for "the day before yesterday".

Weeks, months and years

The same three words work for weeks, months and years: 지난 (last), 이번 (this) and 다음 (next).

MeaningKorean
last week / this week / next week지난주 / 이번 주 / 다음 주
last month / this month / next month지난달 / 이번 달 / 다음 달
last year / this year / next year작년 / 올해 / 내년

Years are the exception: they have their own words, 작년, 올해 and 내년, which you'll hear far more often than the regular pattern.

Parts of the day

  • 오전 (a.m.) and 오후 (p.m.) go before the time: 오후 세 시 is 3 p.m.
  • 아침 is morning, 점심 is midday and 저녁 is evening. All three also mean the meal eaten at that time: breakfast, lunch and dinner.
  • 밤 is night, and 새벽 is the early hours before sunrise.

So 저녁 먹었어요? means "Have you had dinner?", literally "Did you eat evening?".

Asking and saying the date

QuestionMeaning
오늘 며칠이에요?What's the date today?
오늘 무슨 요일이에요?What day is it today?
몇 월이에요?What month is it?
생일이 언제예요?When is your birthday?

The word for "which day of the month" is spelled 며칠, never 몇 일, even though it sounds like it should be. This one trips up Korean writers too.

Dates go from the largest unit to the smallest, with the day of the week at the end: 2026년 11월 7일 토요일, "Saturday, November 7, 2026". In writing, you'll often see the short form 2026. 11. 7. with a period after each number.

On Monday, every Monday

"On" is 에

To say when something happens, add 에 after the time word: 월요일에 (on Monday), 11월에 (in November), 오후 세 시에 (at 3 p.m.). The exceptions are 어제, 오늘 and 내일, which don't take 에: 내일 만나요 (see you tomorrow).

"Every" is 매 or 마다

  • 매일 (every day), 매주 (every week), 매달 (every month), 매년 (every year)
  • With other time words, add 마다: 월요일마다 (every Monday), 아침마다 (every morning)

The four seasons

Korea has four distinct seasons: 봄 (spring), 여름 (summer), 가을 (autumn) and 겨울 (winter). A common small-talk question is 무슨 계절을 좋아해요?, "Which season do you like?".

Practice

Each morning, say the full date in Korean, with the day of the week, and then one thing you'll do: 오늘은 토요일이에요. 오후에 친구를 만나요. ("Today is Saturday. I'm meeting a friend in the afternoon.") It takes a minute and practices numbers, time words and the polite -요 ending together.
