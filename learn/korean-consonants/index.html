Korean Consonants: All 14 Basic Letters, With Sounds

The 14 basic consonants

Korean has 14 basic consonants. Some of them change their sound a little depending on where they appear in a word, so the table shows the romanization at the start of a syllable and at the end of one (as a final consonant, or batchim). Tap the speaker to hear each example word.

Why do so many letters end in "t"? At the end of a syllable, Korean only allows seven sounds, and ㄷ ㅅ ㅈ ㅊ ㅌ ㅎ all end the same way. Our batchim guide explains this.

Learn them in families

Don't memorize the list from top to bottom. Hangul was designed in families: start from five basic shapes that picture the mouth, and add strokes for stronger sounds.

Where in the mouthBasic shapeOne stroke moreTwo strokes more
Back of the tongueㄱ gㅋ k
Tip of the tongueㄴ nㄷ dㅌ t
Lipsㅁ mㅂ bㅍ p
Teethㅅ sㅈ jㅊ ch
Throatㅇ (silent / ng)ㅎ h

That leaves only ㄹ, which has its own shape. If you learn the five basic shapes and the rule "more strokes, stronger sound", you already know 13 of the 14.

Sounds that don't match English

ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ and ㅈ are softer than g, d, b and j

At the start of a word, these four sound somewhere between English g and k, d and t, b and p, and j and ch. They're light and relaxed, with only a little breath. That's why the Korean surname 박 is written both "Bak" and "Park" in English.

Between two vowels they become fully voiced, like English g, d, b and j. Listen to 고기 gogi (meat): the first ㄱ is close to a k, and the second is a clear g. The same happens in 바보 babo (fool).

ㄹ is both r and l

ㄹ sounds like a quick tap of the tongue (like the tt in American "butter") between vowels and at the start of a word: 나라 nara (country). At the end of a syllable, or when two ㄹ meet, it sounds like an English l: 달 dal (moon), 빨리 ppalli (quickly).

ㅅ before ㅣ sounds like "sh"

Before the vowel ㅣ (and the y-vowels like ㅑ and ㅕ), ㅅ is pronounced close to English "sh": 시계 sigye (clock) sounds like "shi-gye". Elsewhere it's an s: 소 so (cow).

ㅇ has two jobs

At the start of a block, ㅇ is silent and only holds the place of the missing consonant: 아이 ai. At the bottom of a block it sounds like the ng in "sing": 공 gong (ball).

Dictionary order

Korean dictionaries, including the word list in our app, sort words by their first consonant in this order. The double consonants (covered in our guide to plain, aspirated and tense consonants) sit right after their single partners:

ㄱ ㄲ ㄴ ㄷ ㄸ ㄹ ㅁ ㅂ ㅃ ㅅ ㅆ ㅇ ㅈ ㅉ ㅊ ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ ㅎ

Korean children learn this order with the syllables 가 나 다 라 마 바 사 아 자 차 카 타 파 하, and you can too: read the row out loud a few times and you've practiced every basic consonant.
