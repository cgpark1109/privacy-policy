Ordering at a Korean Restaurant: Phrases From Door to Bill

2026-11-07 · 4 min read · ggook's studio

Eating out in Korean

Eating out is one of the best ways to start using your Korean. The phrases are short and repeat at every meal, and people are used to helping visitors. This guide follows a meal from the door to the bill. Most requests use one handy ending, 주세요 ("please give me"), so you'll be able to make up your own after a few examples.

Walking in

As you enter, the staff will greet you with 어서 오세요 (welcome). The next question is usually how many are in your group:

  • 몇 분이세요? How many people?
  • 두 명이요. Two, please. (Use native numbers with 명: 한 명, 두 명, 세 명. Our numbers guide explains why.)

In many small restaurants you just pick a free table yourself.

Calling the server

In Korea, servers often won't come to your table until you call them, so don't wait to be noticed:

  • 저기요! Excuse me! Said clearly, with a raised hand, this is completely polite.
  • Many tables have a call button. Press it and someone will come.
  • In small, friendly restaurants, people also call the staff 사장님 or 이모, as explained in our family terms guide.

More and more places also let you order on a kiosk at the entrance or a tablet on the table.

Ordering

PhraseMeaning
메뉴판 주세요.The menu, please.
뭐가 맛있어요?What's good here?
이거 주세요.This one, please. (point at the menu)
비빔밥 하나 주세요.One bibimbap, please.
이거 두 개 주세요.Two of these, please.

Ordering by the portion: 인분

At Korean barbecue and some other places, meat is ordered by the serving, 인분, with Sino-Korean numbers: 삼겹살 2인분 주세요 ("two servings of pork belly, please", read 이 인분). Barbecue restaurants often ask for at least two servings.

Dishes you can already read

If you can read Hangul, you can read the menu. A few popular dishes to look for:

  • 김치찌개: kimchi stew
  • 된장찌개: soybean paste stew
  • 비빔밥: rice mixed with vegetables and chili paste
  • 불고기: marinated grilled beef
  • 냉면: cold noodles
  • 떡볶이: spicy rice cakes

Menu words that help: 찌개 is a stew, 국 or 탕 a soup, 밥 rice, 면 noodles and 고기 meat.

During the meal

Side dishes and water

Most Korean meals come with 반찬, small side dishes like kimchi. They're included with your meal, and in most restaurants you can ask for more without paying extra:

  • 반찬 좀 더 주세요. Could we have more side dishes, please?
  • 김치 좀 더 주세요. More kimchi, please.
  • 물 좀 주세요. Some water, please.

The small word 좀 softens a request, like "please" or "a bit". If you see a sign saying 셀프 ("self"), water or side dishes are self-service: help yourself at the counter or the fridge.

How spicy?

  • 많이 매워요? Is it very spicy?
  • 덜 맵게 해 주세요. Less spicy, please.
  • 안 맵게 해 주세요. Not spicy, please.

Not every dish can be made mild, since the sauce is often prepared in advance, but it's always fine to ask.

Leaving things out

  • 고기 빼고 주세요. Without meat, please. (빼고 means "leaving out".)
  • 저는 땅콩 알레르기가 있어요. I'm allergic to peanuts.
  • 저는 채식주의자예요. I'm vegetarian.

If you don't eat meat or fish, it's worth asking about the broth: many soups and stews that look vegetarian are made with a meat or anchovy stock.

Things you might need

숟가락 (spoon), 젓가락 (chopsticks), 앞접시 (a small plate for sharing) and 물티슈 (wet wipe). Just add 주세요. If you can't see any spoons or chopsticks, look for a drawer in the side of the table or a box on top of it: many restaurants keep them there.

Takeout

  • 포장해 주세요. To go, please.
  • 먹고 갈게요. I'll eat here.

Cafés and fast-food places often ask 드시고 가세요? ("are you eating here?") when you order.

Paying

In many Korean restaurants you don't pay at the table. Take the bill, or just remember your table, and pay at the counter near the door on your way out.

  • 계산해 주세요. The bill, please. (Also what you say at the counter.)
  • 카드 돼요? Can I pay by card?
  • 따로 계산할게요. We'll pay separately.

Tipping isn't part of the culture in Korea, so you pay the price on the menu. As you leave, a 잘 먹었습니다 ("I ate well") and 안녕히 계세요 are a warm way to say thank you; our greetings guide has more.

Putting it together

직원: 어서 오세요. 몇 분이세요?
Server: Welcome. How many people?

손님: 두 명이요. … 저기요! 김치찌개 하나, 비빔밥 하나 주세요.
Customer: Two. … Excuse me! One kimchi stew and one bibimbap, please.

손님: 반찬 좀 더 주세요. … 계산해 주세요.
Customer: More side dishes, please. … The bill, please.
