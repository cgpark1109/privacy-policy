Getting Around in Korea: Subway, Bus and Taxi Phrases

2026-11-10 · 4 min read · ggook's studio

Getting from A to B

Korean cities have some of the easiest public transport in the world: subways run every few minutes, buses go almost everywhere, and taxis are cheap compared with those in many countries. Signs in big cities are usually in English too, but knowing a few Korean words and phrases makes every trip smoother, especially once you leave the main stations. This guide covers the subway, the bus, taxis and asking the way.

Words you'll see everywhere

KoreanMeaning
지하철subway
역station (서울역 is Seoul Station)
호선line (2호선, read 이호선, is Line 2)
출구exit (3번 출구, read 삼 번 출구, is Exit 3)
갈아타는 곳transfer (literally "the place to change")
버스 정류장bus stop
택시taxi

Line and exit numbers use Sino-Korean numbers: 일, 이, 삼. Our numbers guide explains when to use which set.

Paying: transport cards

Most people pay with a rechargeable transport card, which you tap on a reader as you go through the gates or get on the bus. You can buy one, and add money to it, at convenience stores and machines in subway stations. Charging a card is 충전:

  • 교통카드 주세요. A transport card, please.
  • 만 원 충전해 주세요. Please charge 10,000 won.

With a card, you also get a discount when you change between the subway and the bus within a set time, so it pays to tap your card when you get off the bus too. When you transfer, the reader says 환승입니다 ("transfer").

On the subway

Station announcements are made in Korean and English in big cities, and often in Chinese and Japanese as well. Listen for these Korean phrases:

  • 이번 역은 강남역입니다. This stop is Gangnam Station.
  • 다음 역은 시청역입니다. The next station is City Hall Station.
  • 내리실 문은 오른쪽입니다. The doors will open on the right. (왼쪽 is left.)

Platforms are labeled by direction, using the name of the next station or the last stop on the line. Before you go through the gate, check which side you need: at some stations, the two directions have separate entrances.

You'll also see seats in a different color near the ends of each car. These are 노약자석, priority seats for older people, pregnant women and people with disabilities. Many Koreans leave them empty even when the train is crowded.

On the bus

Buses are cheap and cover places the subway doesn't reach, but announcements on smaller routes may be in Korean only. A few phrases help:

  • 이 버스 명동 가요? Does this bus go to Myeong-dong?
  • 어디에서 내려요? Where do I get off?
  • 여기서 내릴게요. I'll get off here.

In most city buses you get on at the front and off at the back, and you press the 하차 ("get off") button before your stop. Map and navigation apps show bus numbers, routes and arrival times, which makes buses much easier to use.

Taking a taxi

To tell a taxi driver where you want to go, say the place and add 가 주세요 ("please go"):

  • 서울역으로 가 주세요. To Seoul Station, please.
  • 이 주소로 가 주세요. To this address, please. (Show the address on your phone.)
  • 여기서 세워 주세요. Please stop here.
  • 카드 돼요? Can I pay by card?

Taxi-hailing apps are also widely used, and let you set the destination without speaking at all.

The particle (으)로 means "to" a place. Use 로 after a vowel or ㄹ (학교로, 서울로) and 으로 after any other consonant (서울역으로, 명동으로). It's the same idea as the particles in our particles guide: a small ending that changes with the sound before it.

Asking the way

When you're lost, 어디예요? ("where is it?") is the key phrase. Put the place in front:

  • 화장실 어디예요? Where's the restroom?
  • 지하철역 어디예요? Where's the subway station?
  • 여기서 멀어요? Is it far from here?

Start with 저기요 ("excuse me"), as explained in our greetings guide, or the more formal 실례합니다. You may hear these words in the answer:

KoreanMeaning
쭉 가세요Go straight on.
오른쪽 / 왼쪽right / left
건너편across the street
가까워요 / 멀어요It's close. / It's far.

Putting it together

관광객: 저기요, 시청역 어디예요?
Tourist: Excuse me, where's City Hall Station?

행인: 쭉 가서 오른쪽이에요. 가까워요.
Passer-by: Go straight and it's on the right. It's close.

관광객: 감사합니다!
Tourist: Thank you!
