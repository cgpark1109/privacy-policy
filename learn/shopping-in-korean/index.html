Shopping in Korean: Asking Prices, Sizes and Paying

2026-11-19 · 4 min read · ggook's studio

From the corner store to the market

Shopping is one of the most rewarding places to use your Korean: the conversations are short, the same phrases come up again and again, and you can always point. This guide covers what you'll hear when you walk in, how to ask for prices and read the answer, how to ask for a different size or color, and how to pay. Most of the phrases use the polite present tense from our verbs guide and the question words from our questions guide.

Walking in

Shop staff will usually greet you with 어서 오세요 ("welcome"). If you're just browsing and someone offers to help, you can say:

  • 그냥 구경하는 거예요. I'm just looking.
  • 이거 있어요? Do you have this? (show a picture on your phone)

How much is it?

The key phrase is 얼마예요? ("how much is it?"). Put the thing in front, or just point:

  • 이거 얼마예요? How much is this?
  • 전부 얼마예요? How much is it altogether?

이거 means "this (near me)", 그거 "that (near you)" and 저거 "that (over there)", so you can point at something across the shop and ask 저거 얼마예요?.

Understanding the price

Korean money is the won, 원, and prices use Sino-Korean numbers. Because Korean groups big numbers by ten thousands (만), as our numbers guide explains, it helps to practice the common amounts:

PriceKorean
1,000 won천 원
3,500 won삼천오백 원
10,000 won만 원
25,000 won이만 오천 원
100,000 won십만 원

Notice that 1,000 and 10,000 are just 천 and 만, with no "one" in front. If you didn't catch the number, it's fine to ask the clerk to show it: many will turn the calculator or the card reader toward you.

Too expensive?

  • 비싸요. It's expensive.
  • 싸요. It's cheap.
  • 좀 깎아 주세요. Could you give me a discount?

In department stores, chain stores and convenience stores the price is fixed, so don't ask for a discount there. In traditional markets and some small shops, a polite 좀 깎아 주세요 is part of the fun, and a seller may round the price down or add a little extra for free. The word for that extra is 서비스: it comes from English "service" but means something given free, "on the house".

Sizes and colors

PhraseMeaning
다른 색 있어요?Do you have it in another color?
더 큰 거 있어요?Do you have a bigger one?
더 작은 거 있어요?Do you have a smaller one?
입어 봐도 돼요?Can I try it on? (clothes)
신어 봐도 돼요?Can I try them on? (shoes)

Korean uses different verbs for wearing different things: 입다 for clothes, 신다 for shoes and socks, 쓰다 for hats and glasses. Clothes sizes are often written in English letters (S, M, L), and shoe sizes in millimeters: a European size 39 is about 245 mm.

A few colors to start with: 검은색 (black), 흰색 (white), 빨간색 (red), 파란색 (blue) and 회색 (gray).

Deciding

  • 이거 주세요. I'll take this one.
  • 이거 두 개 주세요. Two of these, please.
  • 좀 더 생각해 볼게요. I'll think about it a bit more. (A polite way to leave without buying.)

Paying

At the counter, you'll often be asked how you want to pay, or whether you need a bag:

You might hearMeaningYou can answer
카드세요, 현금이세요?Card or cash?카드요. / 현금이요.
봉투 필요하세요?Do you need a bag?네, 주세요. / 아니요, 괜찮아요.
영수증 드릴까요?Would you like a receipt?네, 주세요. / 버려 주세요. (Please throw it away.)

Cards are accepted almost everywhere in Korea, even for small amounts, though a few market stalls prefer cash. Supermarkets and convenience stores usually charge a small amount for a bag (single-use plastic bags have largely been phased out), so many people carry their own. Clothing shops and department stores usually give you a paper bag for free.

Returns and exchanges

  • 교환할 수 있어요? Can I exchange it?
  • 환불해 주세요. I'd like a refund, please.

Keep your receipt: most shops ask for it before they'll exchange or refund anything.

Putting it together

직원: 어서 오세요.
Clerk: Welcome.

손님: 이거 얼마예요?
Customer: How much is this?

직원: 이만 오천 원이에요.
Clerk: It's 25,000 won.

손님: 다른 색 있어요?
Customer: Do you have another color?

직원: 죄송해요, 이 색만 있어요.
Clerk: Sorry, we only have this color.

손님: 그럼 이거 주세요. 카드 돼요?
Customer: Then I'll take this one. Can I pay by card?

Price tags are great number practice: try reading every price you see aloud, or see all guides.
