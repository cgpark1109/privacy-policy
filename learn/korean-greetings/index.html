Everyday Korean Greetings and Phrases for Real Situations

2026-11-07 · 4 min read · ggook's studio

Greetings say more than "hello"

Many Korean greetings are small sentences with a literal meaning: "are you at peace?", "go in peace", "I will eat well". Knowing what they mean makes them easier to remember and helps you understand when each one fits. All the phrases below are in the polite style, which you can use with almost anyone; our politeness guide explains the styles in more detail.

Hello

안녕하세요 (annyeonghaseyo) is the one greeting you need. It means "are you at peace?", and it works at any time of day, with strangers and acquaintances alike. Korean has no everyday equivalent of "good morning" or "good evening", so 안녕하세요 covers them all.

  • In formal situations you'll hear the formal version 안녕하십니까, for example from announcers or at a company event.
  • With close friends and children, just 안녕. It works for both "hi" and "bye".

A small bow or nod of the head usually goes with 안녕하세요, a little deeper for someone older or senior.

Meeting someone for the first time

PhraseMeaning
처음 뵙겠습니다Nice to meet you (formal; literally "I'm seeing you for the first time")
만나서 반가워요Nice to meet you (literally "I'm glad because I met you")
저는 민준이에요I'm Minjun

To introduce yourself, put your name before -이에요 if it ends in a consonant, or before -예요 if it ends in a vowel: 저는 엠마예요 (I'm Emma).

Seeing someone again

  • 오랜만이에요: long time no see
  • 잘 지냈어요?: have you been well? The usual answer is 네, 잘 지냈어요 (yes, I've been well).

Goodbye

Korean has two polite goodbyes, and the choice depends on who is leaving:

PhraseSay it to
안녕히 가세요
"go in peace"		someone who is leaving
안녕히 계세요
"stay in peace"		someone who is staying while you leave

When you walk out of a shop, you're leaving and the staff are staying, so you say 안녕히 계세요. If both of you are leaving, 안녕히 가세요 is the safe choice. Other common goodbyes are 다음에 봐요 (see you next time) and, with friends, 잘 가 (bye, take care).

Thank you and sorry

PhraseMeaningCommon reply
감사합니다
고맙습니다		Thank you아니에요 (it's nothing)
죄송합니다I'm sorry (formal)괜찮아요 (it's OK)
미안해요I'm sorry괜찮아요

감사합니다 and 고맙습니다 mean the same thing and are equally polite. Textbooks often teach 천만에요 for "you're welcome", but in conversation 아니에요 ("not at all") is much more common.

Getting someone's attention

  • 저기요: excuse me. Use it to call a server in a restaurant or to stop someone in the street to ask something.
  • 잠시만요: one moment, or excuse me when you need to get past people on a crowded bus or subway.

At the table

Koreans say a short phrase before and after a meal, especially when someone else cooked or is paying:

  • 잘 먹겠습니다: before eating, literally "I will eat well"
  • 잘 먹었습니다: after eating, literally "I ate well", a way of saying thank you for the meal
  • 맛있게 드세요: enjoy your meal, said to the people who are about to eat

You'll also hear 밥 먹었어요? ("have you eaten?") used as a friendly greeting. It's a way of showing you care, a little like "how are you?", and usually isn't an invitation to lunch.

At work and in shops

  • 어서 오세요: welcome. Shop and restaurant staff say it when you come in. You don't need to reply; a nod or a smile is enough.
  • 수고하셨습니다: literally "you worked hard", said to colleagues at the end of a day or a project to thank them for their effort.
  • 수고하세요: a common thing to say to staff as you leave a shop or office. Some people feel it sounds a little like an order when said to someone senior, so with your boss 안녕히 계세요 is the safer goodbye.

On the phone

Answer the phone with 여보세요. It's mainly used on the phone, where it works like "hello?".

Birthdays and holidays

  • 생일 축하해요: happy birthday
  • 새해 복 많이 받으세요: happy New Year, literally "receive a lot of luck in the new year". It's used both for January 1 and for the Lunar New Year holiday, 설날.
  • 추석 잘 보내세요: have a good Chuseok, the autumn harvest holiday

Practice tip

Greetings are easiest to learn by using them. Pick three phrases from this page, such as 안녕하세요, 감사합니다 and 안녕히 계세요, and use them every chance you get. Listen to the examples here a few times first so the rhythm feels natural, and repeat the greetings you hear in Korean shows and videos.
