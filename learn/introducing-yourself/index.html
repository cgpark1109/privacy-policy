Introducing Yourself in Korean: Name, Country, Job and Hobbies

2026-11-25 · 3 min read · ggook's studio

Your first Korean conversation

A short self-introduction, 자기소개, is often the very first thing you'll say in Korean: in a language class, at work, or when you meet new friends. Korean self-introductions follow a familiar pattern, so once you've prepared yours, you can use it again and again. This guide builds one up step by step, using the polite style from our verbs guide.

Step 1: greeting

Start with 안녕하세요. When you meet someone for the first time, add 처음 뵙겠습니다 (formal) or 만나서 반가워요, both meaning "nice to meet you", as our greetings guide explains. A small bow goes well with it.

Step 2: your name

  • 저는 엠마예요. I'm Emma. (name ends in a vowel: 예요)
  • 저는 제임스예요. I'm James.
  • 제 이름은 민준이에요. My name is Minjun. (name ends in a consonant: 이에요)

저 is the humble word for "I", and 제 means "my". Your name is written in Hangul the way it sounds in Korean, so "James" becomes 제임스, which ends in a vowel. If you're not sure how to write yours, ask a Korean friend or teacher; our How Hangul Works guide explains the letters.

In more formal settings, such as a job interview or a speech, people use the formal ending 입니다 instead: 저는 엠마입니다.

Step 3: where you're from

Add 사람 ("person") after the name of your country:

  • 미국 사람이에요. I'm American.
  • 영국 사람이에요. I'm British.
  • 캐나다에서 왔어요. I'm from Canada. (literally "I came from Canada")

A few country names: 미국 (the US), 영국 (the UK), 호주 (Australia), 독일 (Germany), 프랑스 (France), 일본 (Japan) and 중국 (China). Many others sound close to English: 캐나다, 브라질, 멕시코.

Step 4: your job or studies

KoreanMeaning
학생이에요.I'm a student.
대학생이에요.I'm a university student.
회사원이에요.I'm an office worker.
선생님이에요.I'm a teacher.
회사에 다녀요.I work at a company.
한국어를 공부해요.I'm studying Korean.

A small note on 선생님: it already includes the respectful -님, so it's more modest to say 교사예요 when talking about yourself, especially in formal settings. You'll still often hear 영어 선생님이에요 in casual introductions.

Step 5: hobbies and interests

Use 좋아해요 ("I like") or 취미는 … 예요/이에요 ("my hobby is …"):

  • 음악을 좋아해요. I like music.
  • 취미는 요리예요. My hobby is cooking.
  • 한국 드라마를 자주 봐요. I often watch Korean dramas.
  • 주말에 등산해요. I go hiking on weekends.

Hiking, 등산, is one of the most popular hobbies in Korea, so it's a great way to start a conversation.

Step 6: the polite closing

Korean self-introductions usually end with 잘 부탁드립니다 (or the less formal 잘 부탁해요). It's hard to translate: it means something like "I ask for your kind help" or "I look forward to getting to know you", and it's said with a bow. Ending without it can feel a little abrupt to Korean listeners.

Questions and comments you might hear

After you introduce yourself, people may ask a few questions. Our questions guide covers the question words:

  • 이름이 뭐예요? What's your name?
  • 어느 나라 사람이에요? Which country are you from?
  • 한국에 언제 왔어요? When did you come to Korea?
  • 한국어 잘하시네요! Your Korean is good! A modest answer is 아니에요, 아직 많이 부족해요 ("not at all, I still have a lot to learn").

People may also ask your age, which helps them decide how formally to speak to you, as our politeness guide explains. If you'd rather not say, a smile and a change of subject are fine.

A sample self-introduction

안녕하세요. 처음 뵙겠습니다.
Hello. Nice to meet you.

저는 엠마예요. 호주 사람이에요.
I'm Emma. I'm Australian.

대학생이에요. 한국어를 공부해요.
I'm a university student. I'm studying Korean.

취미는 요리예요. 한국 음식을 정말 좋아해요.
My hobby is cooking. I really love Korean food.

잘 부탁드립니다.
I look forward to getting to know you.
