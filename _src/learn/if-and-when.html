One ending for "if" and "when"

"If it rains, I'll stay home." "When I'm tired, I drink coffee." In Korean, both of these use the same ending, -(으)면, attached to the first verb. It's one of the most useful endings in the language, and once you know it, a lot of everyday phrases suddenly make sense. Like the endings in our connecting sentences guide, it joins two ideas into one sentence.

Making -(으)면

Take the verb stem, then:

  • If the stem ends in a vowel, add -면.
  • If it ends in ㄹ, also just add -면.
  • If it ends in any other consonant, add -으면.
VerbIf / whenMeaning
가다가면{{say:가면}}if (I) go
비싸다비싸면{{say:비싸면}}if it's expensive
만들다만들면{{say:만들면}}if (I) make it
먹다먹으면{{say:먹으면}}if (I) eat
있다있으면{{say:있으면}}if there is, if (I) have

Verbs whose final ㅂ or ㄷ changes in the present tense change before -으면 too: 춥다 → 추우면 ("if it's cold"), 듣다 → 들으면 ("if you listen"). Other present-tense changes don't apply, because -면 goes straight onto the stem: 크다 → 크면, 모르다 → 모르면. With nouns, use 이면 after a consonant and 면 after a vowel: 학생이면 ("if you're a student"), 주말이면 ("if it's the weekend"), 의사면 ("if you're a doctor").

"If": conditions

  • 비가 오면 집에 있을 거예요.{{say:비가 오면 집에 있을 거예요}} If it rains, I'll stay home.
  • 시간이 있으면 같이 가요.{{say:시간이 있으면 같이 가요}} If you have time, let's go together.
  • 너무 비싸면 안 살 거예요.{{say:너무 비싸면 안 살 거예요}} If it's too expensive, I won't buy it.
  • 쭉 가면 오른쪽에 있어요.{{say:쭉 가면 오른쪽에 있어요}} If you go straight on, it's on the right.

To make the "if" clearer, you can start the sentence with 만약{{say:만약}} ("if", "in case"): 만약 늦으면 전화해 주세요{{say:만약 늦으면 전화해 주세요}} ("if you're late, please call me"). It's optional; the -(으)면 already carries the meaning.

"When" and "whenever": habits

-(으)면 also describes what usually happens when something else happens:

  • 피곤하면 커피를 마셔요.{{say:피곤하면 커피를 마셔요}} When I'm tired, I drink coffee.
  • 봄이 되면 꽃이 많이 피어요.{{say:봄이 되면 꽃이 많이 피어요}} When spring comes, lots of flowers bloom.
  • 주말이면 사람이 많아요.{{say:주말이면 사람이 많아요}} On weekends, it's crowded.

For one particular time in the past, like "when I was a child" or "when I went to Seoul", Korean uses a different pattern, -(으)ㄹ 때, in the past tense: 어렸을 때, 서울에 갔을 때. Keep -(으)면 for "if", for things that happen again and again, and for a future "when": 집에 도착하면 전화할게요{{say:집에 도착하면 전화할게요}} ("when I get home, I'll call you").

Tense: no future before -면

As in English "if it rains tomorrow" (not "if it will rain"), the verb before -(으)면 doesn't take the future ending from our future tense guide. The future goes at the end of the sentence instead:

  • 내일 날씨가 좋으면 공원에 갈 거예요.{{say:내일 날씨가 좋으면 공원에 갈 거예요}} If the weather's nice tomorrow, I'll go to the park.

The past tense can go before -면 when something needs to be finished first: 다 먹었으면 가요{{say:다 먹었으면 가요}} ("if you've finished eating, let's go").

Three handy patterns

-(으)면 돼요: "all you need to do is …"

Literally "if you …, it works". It's the natural way to explain what someone should do:

  • 여기서 기다리면 돼요.{{say:여기서 기다리면 돼요}} You can just wait here.
  • 이 버튼을 누르면 돼요.{{say:이 버튼을 누르면 돼요}} You just press this button.
  • 어떻게 하면 돼요?{{say:어떻게 하면 돼요?}} What should I do?

-(으)면 안 돼요: "you mustn't …"

The opposite, "if you …, it's not OK", which you met in our can and can't guide: 여기서 사진 찍으면 안 돼요{{say:여기서 사진 찍으면 안 돼요}} ("you can't take pictures here").

-(으)면 좋겠어요: "I hope …", "I wish …"

Literally "if …, it would be good":

  • 내일 비가 안 오면 좋겠어요.{{say:내일 비가 안 오면 좋겠어요}} I hope it doesn't rain tomorrow.
  • 한국어를 잘했으면 좋겠어요.{{say:한국어를 잘했으면 좋겠어요}} I wish I were good at Korean. (For wishes like this, the past form -았/었으면 is very common.)

Putting it together

가: 이번 주말에 뭐 할 거예요?{{say:이번 주말에 뭐 할 거예요?}}
A: What are you going to do this weekend?

나: 날씨가 좋으면 한강에 가고 싶어요. 비가 오면 집에서 영화 볼 거예요.{{say:날씨가 좋으면 한강에 가고 싶어요. 비가 오면 집에서 영화 볼 거예요}}
B: If the weather's nice, I'd like to go to the Han River. If it rains, I'll watch a movie at home.

가: 저도 시간 있으면 같이 가도 돼요?{{say:저도 시간 있으면 같이 가도 돼요?}}
A: If I have time, can I come too?

나: 그럼요! 토요일 아침에 연락하면 돼요.{{say:그럼요! 토요일 아침에 연락하면 돼요}}
B: Of course! Just get in touch on Saturday morning.

가: 좋아요. 날씨가 좋으면 좋겠어요!{{say:좋아요. 날씨가 좋으면 좋겠어요}}
A: Great. I hope the weather's nice!
