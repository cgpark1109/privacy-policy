Talking about food

Food is one of the favorite topics of conversation in Korea. People ask whether you've eaten, which dishes you like and whether something is too spicy for you. With the words in this guide, you'll be able to answer, and read a lot more of the menu. It builds on our restaurant guide, which covers ordering and paying.

Tasty or not

The most useful food words of all are 맛있어요{{say:맛있어요}} ("it's delicious") and 맛없어요{{say:맛없어요}} ("it doesn't taste good"). They come from 맛 ("taste") with 있어요 ("there is") and 없어요 ("there isn't"). Their pronunciation is a little unusual: 맛있어요 is usually pronounced [마시써요], and 맛없어요 is [마덥써요].

To ask about the taste, say 맛이 어때요?{{say:맛이 어때요?}} ("how does it taste?"). To be polite about food you don't like, 제 입맛에는 좀 안 맞아요{{say:제 입맛에는 좀 안 맞아요}} ("it doesn't quite suit my taste") is gentler than 맛없어요.

Dictionary formPolite formMeaning
맵다매워요{{say:매워요}}spicy, hot
짜다짜요{{say:짜요}}salty
달다달아요{{say:달아요}}sweet
시다셔요{{say:셔요}}sour
쓰다써요{{say:써요}}bitter
싱겁다싱거워요{{say:싱거워요}}bland, not salty enough

맵다 and 싱겁다 follow the same pattern as 춥다 → 추워요 in our weather guide: the ㅂ becomes 워. 시다 becomes 셔요 because 시 + 어요 shortens to 셔요, and 쓰다 becomes 써요 because the ㅡ drops out.

맵다 describes the burn of chili peppers. Spicy dishes are everywhere in Korea, so it's worth knowing 덜 맵게 해 주세요{{say:덜 맵게 해 주세요}} ("please make it less spicy") and 하나도 안 매워요{{say:하나도 안 매워요}} ("it's not spicy at all"), which you may hear from Korean friends about food you find very spicy.

Words with no exact English match

  • 고소해요{{say:고소해요}} (고소하다): a pleasant nutty, toasty flavor, like sesame oil, roasted grain or freshly toasted seaweed.
  • 느끼해요{{say:느끼해요}} (느끼하다): too greasy, rich or oily, like a very creamy pasta. Koreans often eat pickles or kimchi to cut through it.
  • 담백해요{{say:담백해요}} (담백하다): light and clean-tasting, not greasy or strongly seasoned. It's a compliment.
  • 쫄깃해요{{say:쫄깃해요}} (쫄깃하다): pleasantly chewy, like rice cakes or good noodles.
  • 시원해요{{say:시원해요}} (시원하다): "cool" or "refreshing", but also used for hot soup that feels clean and satisfying, as mentioned in our weather guide.

How dishes are named

Many Korean dish names are the main ingredient plus a word for the type of dish. Once you know the endings, you can guess what a lot of menu items are:

볶음{{say:볶음}}stir-fried제육볶음 spicy stir-fried pork

조림{{say:조림}}simmered in a sauce감자조림 soy-braised potatoes

전{{say:전}}pan-fried in batter파전 green onion pancake

The line between 국, 탕 and 찌개 isn't strict, but a 찌개 generally has less broth and stronger seasoning. 비빔, from 비비다 ("to mix"), goes at the front: 비빔밥 is mixed rice, and 비빔국수 is mixed noodles.

The word 고기 combines with animals: 돼지 (pig) + 고기, 닭 (chicken) + 고기. 소고기 is also spelled 쇠고기; both are standard. If you don't eat something, add 빼 주세요 ("please leave it out"): 고기 빼 주세요{{say:고기 빼 주세요}}. Many soups and side dishes are made with anchovy broth or fish sauce, so if you're vegetarian it's worth asking, as our restaurant guide explains.

Hungry and full

  • 배고파요.{{say:배고파요}} I'm hungry.
  • 배불러요.{{say:배불러요}} I'm full. (from 배부르다)
  • 밥 먹었어요?{{say:밥 먹었어요?}} Have you eaten? (often just a friendly greeting)
  • 많이 드세요.{{say:많이 드세요}} Please eat a lot. (said by a host)

밥 먹었어요? is often less a real question than a way of saying "how are you?". A simple 네, 먹었어요 is a fine answer. For mealtime phrases like 잘 먹겠습니다, see our greetings guide.

Putting it together

가: 김치찌개 맛이 어때요? 안 매워요?{{say:김치찌개 맛이 어때요? 안 매워요?}}
A: How's the kimchi stew? Isn't it spicy?

나: 좀 매워요. 그래도 정말 맛있어요!{{say:좀 매워요. 그래도 정말 맛있어요}}
B: It's a bit spicy. But it's really delicious!

가: 파전도 드세요. 고소해요.{{say:파전도 드세요. 고소해요}}
A: Have some green onion pancake too. It's nice and toasty.

나: 감사합니다. 그런데 이제 너무 배불러요.{{say:감사합니다. 그런데 이제 너무 배불러요}}
B: Thank you. But I'm so full now.
