One ending, thousands of sentences

Korean verbs can look intimidating, because they change their endings depending on tense, politeness and much more. The good news is that one form covers a huge amount of everyday conversation: the polite present tense, which ends in -아요 or -어요. You've already seen it in phrases like 맛있어요 ("it's tasty") and 어디예요? ("where is it?"). Once you know how to make it, you can say a surprising amount with very little grammar.

This form is the friendly but polite style explained in our politeness guide. It's the safe choice when talking to people you don't know well.

Verbs in Korean: the basics

  • The verb comes last. Korean word order is subject, object, then verb: 저는 커피를 마셔요{{say:저는 커피를 마셔요}} is literally "I coffee drink".
  • Adjectives act like verbs. Words like "big", "tasty" and "busy" are conjugated just like verbs: 커요 ("it's big"), 바빠요 ("I'm busy").
  • The subject is often left out. 가요 can mean "I go", "you go", "she goes" or "we go", depending on the situation.
  • Questions use the same form. Just raise your voice at the end: 가요. "I'm going." / 가요? "Are you going?"

Step 1: find the stem

In a dictionary, every Korean verb ends in 다. This is the dictionary form. Take off 다 and you have the stem, which is what you add endings to:

Dictionary formStemMeaning
가다가to go
먹다먹to eat
좋다좋to be good
마시다마시to drink

Step 2: choose 아요 or 어요

Look at the last vowel of the stem:

  • If it's ㅏ or ㅗ, add 아요.
  • If it's any other vowel, add 어요.
VerbLast vowelPolite presentMeaning
좋다ㅗ좋아요{{say:좋아요}}It's good.
살다ㅏ살아요{{say:살아요}}I live.
먹다ㅓ먹어요{{say:먹어요}}I eat.
읽다ㅣ읽어요{{say:읽어요}}I read.
맛있다ㅣ맛있어요{{say:맛있어요}}It's tasty.

Notice that in 좋아요 and 먹어요, the final consonant of the stem slides over into the next syllable when you say it, just as our batchim guide describes.

Step 3: contract when the stem ends in a vowel

When the stem ends in a vowel, the two vowels usually merge into one syllable. These contractions are so common that the long forms are almost never used:

Stem ends inExamplePolite presentMeaning
ㅏ (+아 disappears)가다가요{{say:가요}}I go.
ㅓ (+어 disappears)서다서요{{say:서요}}I stand.
ㅗ + 아 → ㅘ오다와요{{say:와요}}I come.
ㅜ + 어 → ㅝ배우다배워요{{say:배워요}}I learn.
ㅣ + 어 → ㅕ마시다마셔요{{say:마셔요}}I drink.
ㅐ (+어 usually disappears)보내다보내요{{say:보내요}}I send.

The combined vowels ㅘ, ㅝ and ㅕ are explained in our vowels guide.

하다 verbs: 해요

Many of the most useful verbs end in 하다 ("to do"): 공부하다 (to study), 일하다 (to work), 좋아하다 (to like), 사랑하다 (to love). They don't follow the rule above. Instead, 하다 always becomes 해요:

  • 공부해요.{{say:공부해요}} I study.
  • 한국어를 좋아해요.{{say:한국어를 좋아해요}} I like Korean.
  • 일해요?{{say:일해요?}} Are you working?

You already know one of these from the restaurant: 계산해 주세요 ("please calculate", the bill) is built on 계산하다.

Two special verbs: 이다 and 있다

  • 이다 ("to be", as in "this is a book") becomes 예요 after a vowel and 이에요 after a consonant: 학생이에요{{say:학생이에요}} "I'm a student", 의사예요{{say:의사예요}} "I'm a doctor".
  • 있다 ("there is", "to have") becomes 있어요, and its opposite 없다 becomes 없어요: 시간 있어요?{{say:시간 있어요?}} "Do you have time?"

Irregular verbs to watch for

A few groups of verbs change their stems in this form. You don't need to learn them all at once, but here are three you'll meet very early:

TypeExamplePolite presentMeaning
Stem ends in ㅡ바쁘다바빠요{{say:바빠요}}I'm busy.
Stem ends in ㅂ맵다매워요{{say:매워요}}It's spicy.
Stem ends in 르모르다몰라요{{say:몰라요}}I don't know.

For ㅡ verbs, the ㅡ drops out and the vowel before it decides between 아 and 어; 크다 ("to be big"), with no vowel before it, becomes 커요. Many verbs ending in ㅂ change it to 워, which is why "spicy" is 매워요, as you saw in the restaurant guide. Not every ㅂ verb does this, though: 입다 ("to wear") is simply 입어요.

Saying "not"

The simplest way to make a sentence negative is to put 안{{say:안}} before the verb:

  • 안 가요.{{say:안 가요}} I'm not going.
  • 안 매워요.{{say:안 매워요}} It's not spicy.

With 하다 verbs made from a noun, 안 goes just before 해요: 공부 안 해요{{say:공부 안 해요}} "I don't study".

Putting it together

가: 지금 뭐 해요?{{say:지금 뭐 해요?}}
A: What are you doing now?

나: 한국어 공부해요. 한국어가 재미있어요.{{say:한국어 공부해요. 한국어가 재미있어요}}
B: I'm studying Korean. Korean is fun.

가: 저도 배워요. 그런데 조금 어려워요.{{say:저도 배워요. 그런데 조금 어려워요}}
A: I'm learning it too. But it's a little hard.
