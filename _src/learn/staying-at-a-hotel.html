Where you'll stay

Korea has every kind of place to stay, from big international hotels to small family-run guesthouses and traditional 한옥{{say:한옥}} houses. Staff at large hotels usually speak English, but at smaller places a little Korean goes a long way. The general word for a place to stay is 숙소{{say:숙소}}. This guide follows a stay from check-in to check-out.

Hotel words are a good place to practice reading: 체크인, 프런트 and 카드 키 are English words written in Hangul. If you can't read them yet, start with How Hangul Works.

Checking in

At the desk, tell them you have a booking and give your name:

  • 체크인하려고요.{{say:체크인하려고요}} I'd like to check in.
  • 예약했어요.{{say:예약했어요}} I have a reservation. (literally "I booked.")
  • 존 스미스로 예약했어요.{{say:존 스미스로 예약했어요}} It's under John Smith.

The staff will probably say 여권 보여 주세요{{say:여권 보여 주세요}} ("please show me your passport"). In 존 스미스로, the particle (으)로 means "under" or "in the name of". It's the same particle as in 아이스로 주세요 in our café guide.

Room numbers and floors

Room numbers end in 호{{say:호}}, and floors in 층{{say:층}}. Both use Sino-Korean numbers, as explained in our numbers guide:

  • 302호예요.{{say:삼백이 호예요}} It's room 302. (read 삼백이 호)
  • 방이 몇 층이에요?{{say:방이 몇 층이에요?}} What floor is the room on?
  • 3층이에요.{{say:삼 층이에요}} It's on the third floor. (read 삼 층)

Floor numbers in Korea count the ground floor as 1층, as in the United States, so 2층 is one floor up. Basement floors start with 지하{{say:지하}} ("underground"), and elevator buttons show them as B1, B2. In some buildings the elevator shows F (for "four") instead of 4, because the Sino-Korean number four, 사, sounds the same as the Sino-Korean word for death.

Questions to ask

  • 조식은 몇 시예요?{{say:조식은 몇 시예요?}} What time is breakfast?
  • 체크아웃은 몇 시예요?{{say:체크아웃은 몇 시예요?}} What time is check-out?
  • 와이파이 비밀번호 뭐예요?{{say:와이파이 비밀번호 뭐예요?}} What's the Wi-Fi password?
  • 짐 맡길 수 있어요?{{say:짐 맡길 수 있어요?}} Can I leave my luggage here? (맡기다: to leave something in someone's care)
  • 근처에 편의점 있어요?{{say:근처에 편의점 있어요?}} Is there a convenience store nearby?

-(으)ㄹ 수 있어요? means "can I …?" or "is it possible to …?". You've seen it in 교환할 수 있어요? ("can I exchange it?") in our shopping guide. Luggage storage is common: many places will keep your bags before check-in or after check-out.

When something's wrong

The phrase 안 돼요 ("it doesn't work") from our guide to saying no is very useful here. Put the thing that's broken in front, with 이/가:

  • 에어컨이 안 돼요.{{say:에어컨이 안 돼요}} The air conditioning isn't working.
  • 와이파이가 안 돼요.{{say:와이파이가 안 돼요}} The Wi-Fi isn't working.
  • 뜨거운 물이 안 나와요.{{say:뜨거운 물이 안 나와요}} There's no hot water. (literally "hot water doesn't come out")
  • 방이 너무 추워요.{{say:방이 너무 추워요}} The room is too cold.
  • 수건 좀 더 주세요.{{say:수건 좀 더 주세요}} Could I have some more towels, please?
  • 방 바꿀 수 있어요?{{say:방 바꿀 수 있어요?}} Could I change rooms?

If you've locked yourself out, say 카드 키를 방에 두고 나왔어요{{say:카드 키를 방에 두고 나왔어요}} ("I left my key card in the room").

Ondol rooms and guesthouses

Some guesthouses, 한옥 stays and even hotels offer an 온돌방{{say:온돌방}}, a room where you sleep on bedding spread on a heated floor. 온돌 is Korea's traditional underfloor heating, and most Korean homes today still have heated floors. The quilt or comforter is 이불{{say:이불}}, and the thin mattress laid on the floor is 요{{say:요}}. As in Korean homes, take your shoes off before you step onto the floor.

Guesthouses often have shared spaces, so you may see signs like 공용 주방 (shared kitchen) and 공용 욕실 (shared bathroom), or 조용히 해 주세요 ("please keep quiet") after a certain time at night.

Checking out

  • 체크아웃할게요.{{say:체크아웃할게요}} I'd like to check out.
  • 택시 불러 주세요.{{say:택시 불러 주세요}} Could you call a taxi for me?
  • 잘 쉬었어요. 감사합니다!{{say:잘 쉬었어요. 감사합니다}} I had a good rest. Thank you!

Tipping isn't customary in Korea, at hotels or elsewhere, so there's no need to leave money in the room.

Putting it together

손님: 안녕하세요. 체크인하려고요. 존 스미스로 예약했어요.{{say:안녕하세요. 체크인하려고요. 존 스미스로 예약했어요}}
Guest: Hello. I'd like to check in. It's under John Smith.

직원: 네, 여권 보여 주세요.{{say:네, 여권 보여 주세요}}
Clerk: Sure, may I see your passport?

직원: 302호입니다. 조식은 일곱 시부터 열 시까지예요.{{say:삼백이 호입니다. 조식은 일곱 시부터 열 시까지예요}}
Clerk: You're in room 302. Breakfast is from seven to ten.

손님: 와이파이 비밀번호 뭐예요?{{say:와이파이 비밀번호 뭐예요?}}
Guest: What's the Wi-Fi password?

직원: 카드 키 봉투에 있어요.{{say:카드 키 봉투에 있어요}}
Clerk: It's on the key card envelope.

부터 means "from" and 까지 means "until". For getting to your hotel, see our getting around guide, or see all guides.
