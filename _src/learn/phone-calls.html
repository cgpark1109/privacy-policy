Why phone calls feel hard

Phone calls are often the most stressful part of using a new language: you can't see the other person's face, you can't point, and the sound isn't always clear. The good news is that phone conversations in Korean follow a few fixed patterns. Learn the phrases in this guide, and you'll know what to expect at the start and end of every call.

Answering: 여보세요

Calls usually start with 여보세요{{say:여보세요}}, which works like "hello?" and, as our greetings guide explains, is mainly used on the phone. Then the caller says who they are, or checks who they've reached:

  • 여보세요? 저 민수예요.{{say:여보세요? 저 민수예요}} Hello? It's Minsu.
  • 안녕하세요, 한국호텔이죠?{{say:안녕하세요, 한국호텔이죠?}} Hello, is this Hanguk Hotel?

The ending -죠? in 호텔이죠? checks something you think is true, like "this is …, isn't it?". If you don't recognize the caller, ask 누구세요?{{say:누구세요?}} ("who is this?"), or more politely, 실례지만 누구세요?{{say:실례지만 누구세요?}} ("excuse me, but who's calling?").

Asking for someone

To ask for a particular person, use 바꿔 주세요, literally "please change", which on the phone means "please put … on":

  • 민지 씨 있어요?{{say:민지 씨 있어요?}} Is Minji there?
  • 김민지 씨 좀 바꿔 주세요.{{say:김민지 씨 좀 바꿔 주세요}} Could I speak to Kim Minji, please?

You might hear these answers:

KoreanMeaning
잠깐만요.{{say:잠깐만요}}Just a moment.
잠시만 기다려 주세요.{{say:잠시만 기다려 주세요}}Please hold on a moment. (more formal)
지금 자리에 안 계세요.{{say:지금 자리에 안 계세요}}They're not at their desk right now.
메시지 남기시겠어요?{{say:메시지 남기시겠어요?}}Would you like to leave a message?

계세요 is the honorific form of 있어요, used about someone you're showing respect to. Our politeness guide explains it.

Reading phone numbers

Phone numbers are read digit by digit with Sino-Korean numbers, and zero is 공, as our numbers guide explains. Korean mobile numbers start with 010:

  • 010-1234-5678: 공일공, 일이삼사, 오육칠팔{{say:공일공, 일이삼사, 오육칠팔}}

You'll often hear [에] in place of the hyphens: [공일공에 일이삼사에 오육칠팔]. It's the particle 의, which is often pronounced [에]. To ask for someone's number, say 전화번호가 뭐예요?{{say:전화번호가 뭐예요?}} or 전화번호 좀 알려 주세요{{say:전화번호 좀 알려 주세요}} ("could you give me your phone number?").

When you can't understand

Don't be afraid to ask the other person to repeat or slow down. These phrases will get you through most calls:

  • 잘 안 들려요.{{say:잘 안 들려요}} I can't hear you very well.
  • 다시 한번 말씀해 주세요.{{say:다시 한번 말씀해 주세요}} Could you say that again, please?
  • 조금 천천히 말씀해 주세요.{{say:조금 천천히 말씀해 주세요}} Could you speak a little more slowly, please?
  • 한국어를 잘 못해요. 문자로 보내 주실 수 있어요?{{say:한국어를 잘 못해요. 문자로 보내 주실 수 있어요?}} My Korean isn't very good. Could you send it by text?

말씀하다 is a respectful word for "to speak", used about the other person. Asking for a text message is a great trick for addresses, times and numbers. Our texting guide will help you read the reply.

Wrong numbers

  • 잘못 거셨어요.{{say:잘못 거셨어요}} You have the wrong number.
  • 죄송합니다, 잘못 걸었어요.{{say:죄송합니다, 잘못 걸었어요}} Sorry, I dialed the wrong number.

Here 잘못, written as one word, means "wrongly", the same word as in 잘못했어요 in our guide to saying no. 걸다 means "to make a call", and its opposite, 받다, means "to answer": 전화를 안 받아요{{say:전화를 안 받아요}} ("they're not answering").

Calling a restaurant or shop

When you call to book something, it's natural to start with a softened sentence ending in -(으)ㄴ데요/-는데요. You can learn these as set phrases:

  • 예약하고 싶은데요.{{say:예약하고 싶은데요}} I'd like to make a reservation.
  • 토요일 저녁 일곱 시에 두 명이요.{{say:토요일 저녁 일곱 시에 두 명이요}} Two people, at seven on Saturday evening.
  • 몇 시까지 해요?{{say:몇 시까지 해요?}} What time are you open until?

Our making plans guide covers telling the time, the days and dates guide covers days of the week, and the restaurant guide covers counting people with 명.

Ending the call

Korean phone calls often end quickly, with one of these:

  • 네, 알겠습니다.{{say:네, 알겠습니다}} OK, understood.
  • 나중에 다시 전화할게요.{{say:나중에 다시 전화할게요}} I'll call you back later.
  • 끊을게요.{{say:끊을게요}} I'll hang up now. (끊다: to cut, to hang up; with people you know well)
  • 들어가세요.{{say:들어가세요}} Goodbye. (literally "please go in")

들어가세요 is an everyday way to say goodbye at the end of a call, especially to someone older. It comes from wishing someone a safe trip home, and it's used even when they're already at home. You'll also hear 안녕히 계세요 on the phone.

In an emergency, call 119 for an ambulance or the fire service, or 112 for the police. Our pharmacy guide has more.

Putting it together

직원: 여보세요, 서울식당입니다.{{say:여보세요, 서울식당입니다}}
Staff: Hello, Seoul Restaurant.

손님: 안녕하세요. 예약하고 싶은데요. 토요일 저녁 일곱 시에 두 명이요.{{say:안녕하세요. 예약하고 싶은데요. 토요일 저녁 일곱 시에 두 명이요}}
Caller: Hello. I'd like to make a reservation. Two people, at seven on Saturday evening.

직원: 네, 성함이 어떻게 되세요?{{say:네, 성함이 어떻게 되세요?}}
Staff: Certainly. May I have your name?

손님: 존 스미스예요. 전화번호는 공일공, 일이삼사, 오육칠팔이에요.{{say:존 스미스예요. 전화번호는 공일공, 일이삼사, 오육칠팔이에요}}
Caller: John Smith. My phone number is 010-1234-5678.

직원: 네, 예약됐습니다. 감사합니다.{{say:네, 예약됐습니다. 감사합니다}}
Staff: All right, you're booked. Thank you.

성함 is a respectful word for "name"; 성함이 어떻게 되세요? is how staff politely ask yours. See all guides for more.
