Korean on your phone

Once you start chatting with Korean friends, you'll quickly notice messages full of letters on their own: ㅋㅋㅋ, ㅠㅠ, ㅇㅇ. Korean texting uses lots of these shortcuts, and they're a fun way to see how Hangul works, because many of them are just the first consonants of words. Most Koreans chat on messaging apps rather than by SMS, but the style is the same everywhere.

This guide is about casual chat between friends. In messages to teachers, colleagues or people you don't know well, write in full, polite sentences.

Laughing: ㅋㅋ and ㅎㅎ

  • ㅋㅋㅋ{{say:크크크}}: the most common way to laugh in text, from the sound 크크, a bit like "haha" or "lol". The more ㅋs, the funnier.
  • ㅎㅎ{{say:흐흐}}: a softer laugh or a friendly smile, from 하하 or 흐흐. Many people add it to sound warm rather than to show something is funny.

Our consonants guide covers ㅋ and ㅎ; these chat laughs are good practice for recognizing them.

Crying and feelings: ㅠㅠ and ㅜㅜ

ㅠㅠ and ㅜㅜ look like crying eyes with tears running down. They show sadness, disappointment, or being moved, but they're also used playfully: 배고파요 ㅠㅠ{{say:배고파요}} ("I'm hungry"). The vowels themselves are explained in our vowels guide.

Consonant abbreviations

Many common replies are shortened to just their first consonants:

TextShort forMeaning
ㅇㅇ응응{{say:응응}}yeah, OK (casual)
ㅇㅋ오케이{{say:오케이}}OK (from English "okay")
ㄴㄴ노노{{say:노노}}no, no (from English "no")
ㄱㅅ감사{{say:감사}}thanks
ㅈㅅ죄송{{say:죄송}}sorry
ㅊㅋ축하{{say:축하}}congrats
ㄱㄱ고고{{say:고고}}let's go (from English "go go")
ㅂㅂ바이바이{{say:바이바이}}bye-bye

These are very casual. Use them only with close friends of a similar age, and avoid ㄱㅅ and ㅈㅅ for real thanks or apologies, which can seem careless. Our greetings guide has the full phrases.

Shortened words

Koreans also shorten longer words and phrases, often by taking a syllable from each part:

  • 카톡{{say:카톡}}: a message on a popular chat app, also used as a verb, 카톡할게요 ("I'll message you")
  • 생파{{say:생파}}: birthday party (생일 파티)
  • 맛집{{say:맛집}}: a really good restaurant (맛 "taste" + 집 "house")
  • 알바{{say:알바}}: a part-time job (from 아르바이트, from German Arbeit)
  • 셀카{{say:셀카}}: a selfie (셀프 카메라)

Unlike the consonant abbreviations, many of these are everyday words that you'll hear in speech too.

Politeness in messages

The politeness rules apply in texts just as in speech. With friends of the same age, people use casual speech without -요. With most other people, keep the polite -요 endings:

  • To a friend: 어디야? ㅋㅋ{{say:어디야?}} ("where are you?")
  • To a colleague: 어디세요?{{say:어디세요?}} ("where are you?")

A few habits soften polite messages and make them feel friendlier: adding ~ at the end (감사합니다~), a ㅎㅎ, or an emoticon. A message that ends with just a period can sometimes feel a little cold.

Emoticons

Korean emoticons are usually read straight on, without tilting your head, and focus on the eyes:

  • ^^ or ^_^: smiling
  • ㅠ_ㅠ or T_T: crying
  • -_-: unimpressed
  • ;;: a nervous sweat, "awkward…"

Chat apps also have huge collections of stickers, and sending one is a common way to reply.

Putting it together
