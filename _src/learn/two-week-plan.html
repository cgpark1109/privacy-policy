How to use this plan

You don't need long study sessions to learn Hangul. Fifteen to twenty minutes a day is enough, and short daily practice works better than one long session a week. Each day below has a small goal. If a day feels easy, move on; if it feels hard, repeat it. Two weeks is a guide, not a deadline.

Three habits will help the whole way through:

  • Write by hand. Writing each new letter a few times, in the right stroke order, helps it stick far better than only looking at it.
  • Say everything out loud. Listen to each example, then repeat it. Your mouth needs practice too.
  • Leave romanization behind early. Use it on the first days, then cover it up and read the Hangul on its own.

Week 1: the basic letters

Day 1: five shapes and three vowels

Learn the five basic consonants ㄱ ㄴ ㅁ ㅅ ㅇ and what they picture (see How Hangul Works). Add the vowels ㅏ, ㅗ and ㅣ, and read your first blocks: 가 나 마 사 아 고 노 모 소 오.

Day 2: the rest of the simple vowels

Add ㅓ ㅜ ㅡ and practice the tricky pairs 어/오 and 우/으 (see Korean Vowels). Read 우유{{say:우유}} (milk), 아이{{say:아이}} (child) and 오이{{say:오이}} (cucumber).

Day 3: add strokes, get new consonants

Learn ㄷ ㅌ, ㅂ ㅍ and ㅈ ㅊ by adding strokes to ㄴ, ㅁ and ㅅ. Read 바다{{say:바다}} (sea), 포도{{say:포도}} (grapes) and 지도{{say:지도}} (map).

Day 4: the last basic consonants

Finish with ㅋ, ㅎ and ㄹ. Read the whole row 가 나 다 라 마 바 사 아 자 차 카 타 파 하{{say:가 나 다 라 마 바 사 아 자 차 카 타 파 하}} out loud a few times (see Korean Consonants).

Day 5: y-vowels

One extra stroke adds a y: ㅑ ㅕ ㅛ ㅠ. Read 야구{{say:야구}} (baseball), 여자{{say:여자}} (woman), 요리{{say:요리}} (cooking) and 유리{{say:유리}} (glass).

Day 6: build blocks and write your name

Practice placing tall and flat vowels in blocks. Then write your name in Hangul. Korean writes foreign names by sound, so Emma becomes 엠마, Sophia becomes 소피아 and David becomes 데이비드. Don't worry about getting it perfect: the goal is to use the letters.

Day 7: review

Go back over the 14 consonants and 10 vowels. Make a list of every letter you hesitate on and spend a few extra minutes on those.

Week 2: from letters to real words

Days 8 and 9: plain, aspirated and tense

Learn the double consonants ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ ㅆ ㅉ and practice hearing the three kinds of consonants with the word sets in Plain, Aspirated and Tense. Expect this to take a while: it's normal.

Days 10 and 11: compound vowels

Learn the 11 compound vowels by looking at their parts, and remember that ㅐ and ㅔ sound the same in everyday speech. Read 사과{{say:사과}} (apple), 회사{{say:회사}} (company) and 의자{{say:의자}} (chair).

Day 12: batchim

Learn the seven end sounds in Batchim. Read 밥{{say:밥}} (rice), 꽃{{say:꽃}} (flower) and 강{{say:강}} (river).

Day 13: linking and nasal sounds

Practice the two changes you'll hear most: linking (음악{{say:음악}} sounds like 으막) and k/t/p turning nasal (감사합니다{{say:감사합니다}} sounds like 감사함니다).

Day 14: read the real world

Test yourself on words you'd see in Korea. Loanwords from English are great practice, because once you've read them you'll recognize the meaning:
