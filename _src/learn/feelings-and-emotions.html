How are you feeling?

Being able to say how you feel makes conversations warmer and more personal. Korean has a rich set of words for feelings, and most of them work like adjectives: you conjugate them just like verbs, as in our present tense guide. This guide covers the most useful ones and a few cultural points along the way.

Asking about feelings

  • 기분이 어때요?{{say:기분이 어때요?}} How are you feeling? (기분: mood, feeling)
  • 괜찮아요?{{say:괜찮아요?}} Are you OK?
  • 무슨 일 있어요?{{say:무슨 일 있어요?}} Is something wrong? (literally "is there some matter?")

The answers can be as simple as 기분이 좋아요{{say:기분이 좋아요}} ("I'm in a good mood") or 기분이 안 좋아요{{say:기분이 안 좋아요}} ("I'm not in a good mood").

배고프다배고파요{{say:배고파요}}I'm hungry.
심심하다심심해요{{say:심심해요}}I'm bored.
긴장되다긴장돼요{{say:긴장돼요}}I'm nervous.
화나다화나요{{say:화나요}}I'm angry.
걱정되다걱정돼요{{say:걱정돼요}}I'm worried.
외롭다외로워요{{say:외로워요}}I'm lonely.

Several of these follow the irregular patterns from our verbs guide: 기쁘다, 슬프다 and 배고프다 drop their ㅡ (기뻐요, 슬퍼요, 배고파요), and 외롭다 changes ㅂ to 워 (외로워요). 긴장되다 and 걱정되다 end in 되다, which becomes 돼요.

The difference between 기뻐요 and 행복해요 is a bit like "glad" and "happy": 기뻐요 is joy about something that just happened, while 행복해요 is a deeper sense of happiness.

Adding degree

  • 조금{{say:조금}} or 좀: a little: 좀 피곤해요 I'm a bit tired.
  • 너무{{say:너무}}: very, too: 너무 심심해요 I'm so bored.
  • 정말{{say:정말}} or 진짜{{say:진짜}}: really: 정말 기뻐요 I'm really happy.

Talking about other people's feelings

Korean treats feelings as something private: you can know how you feel, but you can only see signs of how someone else feels. So when talking about another person (not "you" in a question), Koreans usually change the adjective into a verb with -아하다/-어하다, meaning "shows that they feel …":

  • 저는 기뻐요. I'm happy. → 동생이 기뻐해요.{{say:동생이 기뻐해요}} My sibling is happy.
  • 저는 슬퍼요. I'm sad. → 친구가 슬퍼해요.{{say:친구가 슬퍼해요}} My friend is sad.

You've already met one word made this way: 좋아하다 ("to like") comes from 좋다 ("to be good"). That's why it's 한국어가 좋아요 ("Korean is good, to me") but 한국어를 좋아해요 ("I like Korean").

Words with no exact English match

  • 답답하다{{say:답답하다}}: a stifling, frustrated feeling, as when you can't explain yourself, or when a room is stuffy. 답답해요! is something learners often feel when they can't find the right word.
  • 아쉽다{{say:아쉽다}}: a feeling of regret that something is over or didn't quite happen, like a holiday ending or a near miss. 아쉬워요.
  • 설레다{{say:설레다}}: a fluttery, excited feeling before something good, like a trip or a date. 설레요.
  • 시원하다{{say:시원하다}}: refreshingly cool, but also the relief of finally getting something off your chest. Our weather guide explains its other uses.

Responding kindly

  • 힘내세요!{{say:힘내세요}} Cheer up! / Hang in there! (literally "put out your strength")
  • 괜찮을 거예요.{{say:괜찮을 거예요}} It'll be OK.
  • 축하해요!{{say:축하해요}} Congratulations!

괜찮을 거예요 uses the future ending from our future tense guide to mean "it'll probably be fine".

Putting it together

가: 무슨 일 있어요? 기분이 안 좋아 보여요.{{say:무슨 일 있어요? 기분이 안 좋아 보여요}}
A: Is something wrong? You look like you're in a bad mood.

나: 내일 시험이 있어서 너무 긴장돼요.{{say:내일 시험이 있어서 너무 긴장돼요}}
B: I have an exam tomorrow, so I'm really nervous.

가: 열심히 공부했잖아요. 괜찮을 거예요. 힘내세요!{{say:열심히 공부했잖아요. 괜찮을 거예요. 힘내세요}}
A: You studied hard. It'll be OK. You can do it!
