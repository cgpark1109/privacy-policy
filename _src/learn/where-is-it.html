Finding things

"Where's my phone?" "It's on the table." "The bank is next to the café." Saying where something is comes up all the time, and Korean does it with a simple pattern that you can learn in one sitting. This guide covers the words for here and there, the main location words, and how to put them into a sentence with 있어요.

Here, there and over there

Korean has three words where English has two:

KoreanMeaningAlso
여기{{say:여기}}here (near me)이거 this (thing)
거기{{say:거기}}there (near you, or a place we just talked about)그거 that (thing)
저기{{say:저기}}over there (away from both of us)저거 that (thing over there)

The same three sounds, 이, 그 and 저, also go in front of nouns: 이 식당 (this restaurant), 그 사람 (that person), 저 건물 (that building over there). You've already met 이거 주세요 ("this one, please") in our restaurant guide, and 저기요 ("excuse me", which comes from 저기) in our greetings guide.

The location words

KoreanMeaning
위{{say:위}}on, above, top
아래 / 밑{{say:아래, 밑}}under, below
앞{{say:앞}}in front of
뒤{{say:뒤}}behind, back
옆{{say:옆}}next to, beside
안{{say:안}}in, inside
밖{{say:밖}}outside
사이{{say:사이}}between
근처{{say:근처}}near, nearby

아래 and 밑 both mean "under", and in most everyday sentences either one works; 밑 is especially common for something directly underneath, like 침대 밑 (under the bed). Note that 안 ("inside") is spelled and pronounced just like the 안 that means "not" in our guide to saying no. The position in the sentence tells you which one it is: "inside" comes after a noun, and "not" comes before a verb.

The pattern: noun + location word + 에 있어요

In English, the location word comes first: "on the table". In Korean, the order is reversed: first the reference point, then the location word, then 에 ("at"), then 있어요 ("is"):

  • 책상 위에 있어요.{{say:책상 위에 있어요}} It's on the desk. (literally "desk top-at is")
  • 가방 안에 있어요.{{say:가방 안에 있어요}} It's in the bag.
  • 침대 밑에 있어요.{{say:침대 밑에 있어요}} It's under the bed.
  • 은행은 카페 옆에 있어요.{{say:은행은 카페 옆에 있어요}} The bank is next to the café.
  • 화장실은 엘리베이터 뒤에 있어요.{{say:화장실은 엘리베이터 뒤에 있어요}} The restroom is behind the elevator.

For "between", name both places with 하고 ("and"): 편의점은 약국하고 은행 사이에 있어요{{say:편의점은 약국하고 은행 사이에 있어요}} ("the convenience store is between the pharmacy and the bank").

To say something isn't there, use 없어요: 가방 안에 없어요{{say:가방 안에 없어요}} ("it's not in the bag"). And to ask where something is, use 어디에 있어요?{{say:어디에 있어요?}}: 제 휴대폰 어디에 있어요? ("where's my phone?"). In speech, 어디 있어요? without 에 is very common too, as is the shorter 어디예요? from our getting around guide.

에 or 에서?

Both particles can mean "at" or "in" a place, but they're used differently:

  • 에 marks where something is (with 있어요 and 없어요) or where you're going: 학교에 있어요 ("I'm at school"), 학교에 가요 ("I'm going to school").
  • 에서 marks where an action happens: 학교에서 공부해요{{say:학교에서 공부해요}} ("I study at school").

So with location words: 카페 앞에 있어요 ("I'm in front of the café"), but 카페 앞에서 만나요{{say:카페 앞에서 만나요}} ("let's meet in front of the café"), because meeting is an action. You saw this in our making plans guide: 출구에서 만나요.

Moving: 앞으로, 위로

Add (으)로 to a location word to show a direction of movement: 앞으로 가세요{{say:앞으로 가세요}} ("go forward"), 위로 올라가세요{{say:위로 올라가세요}} ("go up"), 밖으로 나가세요{{say:밖으로 나가세요}} ("go outside"). Use 으로 after a consonant other than ㄹ, and 로 after a vowel or ㄹ, as in 위로.

Putting it together

가: 제 휴대폰 어디에 있어요? 책상 위에 없어요.{{say:제 휴대폰 어디에 있어요? 책상 위에 없어요}}
A: Where's my phone? It's not on the desk.

나: 소파 위에 없어요? 아까 거기 있었어요.{{say:소파 위에 없어요? 아까 거기 있었어요}}
B: Isn't it on the sofa? It was there a little while ago.

가: 네, 없어요.{{say:네, 없어요}}
A: No, it's not there. (네 agrees with "it isn't there", as explained in our guide to saying no.)

나: 그럼 가방 안에 있을 거예요.{{say:그럼 가방 안에 있을 거예요}}
B: Then it'll be in your bag.

가: 아, 여기 있어요! 고마워요.{{say:아, 여기 있어요! 고마워요}}
A: Oh, here it is! Thanks.

있을 거예요 is a guess, as explained in our future tense guide. Try describing where five things in your room are, or see all guides.
