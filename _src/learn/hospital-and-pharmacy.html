When you're not feeling well

Nobody plans to get sick while traveling or living abroad, but a cold, a stomachache or a twisted ankle can happen to anyone. In Korea, pharmacies are easy to find, and small neighborhood clinics usually see you quickly and without an appointment. A few Korean words will help you explain what's wrong. This guide uses the polite present tense from our verbs guide.

In an emergency, call 119 for an ambulance or the fire service. Interpretation in English and some other languages is available on request. Police are 112. This guide is for everyday situations and is not medical advice.

Saying where it hurts

The key word is 아파요{{say:아파요}} ("it hurts", "I'm sick"), from 아프다. Put the body part in front, with 이/가:

KoreanMeaning
머리가 아파요.{{say:머리가 아파요}}I have a headache. (머리: head)
배가 아파요.{{say:배가 아파요}}I have a stomachache. (배: stomach, belly)
목이 아파요.{{say:목이 아파요}}I have a sore throat. (목: throat, neck)
이가 아파요.{{say:이가 아파요}}I have a toothache. (이: tooth)
허리가 아파요.{{say:허리가 아파요}}My lower back hurts. (허리: lower back, waist)
다리가 아파요.{{say:다리가 아파요}}My leg hurts. (다리: leg)

If you don't know the word, point and say 여기가 아파요{{say:여기가 아파요}} ("it hurts here"). Our particles guide explains why the body part takes 이/가.

Common symptoms

  • 감기에 걸렸어요.{{say:감기에 걸렸어요}} I've caught a cold.
  • 열이 나요.{{say:열이 나요}} I have a fever.
  • 기침을 해요.{{say:기침을 해요}} I have a cough.
  • 콧물이 나요.{{say:콧물이 나요}} I have a runny nose. (콧물 is pronounced [콘물].)
  • 어지러워요.{{say:어지러워요}} I feel dizzy.
  • 설사를 해요.{{say:설사를 해요}} I have diarrhea.
  • 토할 것 같아요.{{say:토할 것 같아요}} I feel like I'm going to throw up.
  • 발목을 삐었어요.{{say:발목을 삐었어요}} I've sprained my ankle.

Some of these use the past tense, like 걸렸어요 ("I caught") and 삐었어요 ("I sprained"). You can learn them as set phrases for now. To say how long something has lasted, add a starting point with 부터 ("since"): 어제부터 열이 나요{{say:어제부터 열이 나요}} ("I've had a fever since yesterday").

At the pharmacy

A pharmacy is 약국{{say:약국}}, and medicine is 약{{say:약}}. Look for a sign with a large 약 (sometimes the Chinese character 藥). Pharmacists can sell many common medicines without a prescription and will often ask about your symptoms.

  • 감기약 주세요.{{say:감기약 주세요}} Cold medicine, please.
  • 두통약 있어요?{{say:두통약 있어요?}} Do you have something for a headache?
  • 소화제 주세요.{{say:소화제 주세요}} Something for indigestion, please.
  • 하루에 몇 번 먹어요?{{say:하루에 몇 번 먹어요?}} How many times a day do I take it?

In Korean, you "eat" medicine: 약을 먹어요. You'll often hear instructions like 하루에 세 번, 식후 30분에 드세요{{say:하루에 세 번, 식후 삼십 분에 드세요}} ("three times a day, thirty minutes after meals"). 드세요 is the polite way to say "please eat" or "please take". Some basic medicines are also sold at convenience stores, which can help late at night.

At a clinic

For a minor illness, most people go to a small local clinic, 의원{{say:의원}}, rather than a hospital, 병원{{say:병원}} (people often use 병원 for both). Clinics usually specialize, and the sign tells you which kind it is:

KoreanKind of clinic
내과{{say:내과}}internal medicine: colds, fevers, stomach problems
이비인후과{{say:이비인후과}}ear, nose and throat
정형외과{{say:정형외과}}orthopedics: bones, joints, sprains
치과{{say:치과}}dental clinic
피부과{{say:피부과}}dermatology: skin

At the reception desk, you'll usually be asked for your name and some ID; foreign visitors may be asked for a passport. Useful phrases:

  • 진료받고 싶어요.{{say:진료받고 싶어요}} I'd like to see a doctor.
  • 영어 하는 의사 선생님 계세요?{{say:영어 하는 의사 선생님 계세요?}} Is there a doctor who speaks English?
  • 얼마나 기다려야 돼요?{{say:얼마나 기다려야 돼요?}} How long do I have to wait?

After seeing the doctor, you'll get a prescription, 처방전{{say:처방전}}, and take it to a pharmacy, which is often right next door or downstairs.

Things the doctor may ask

Doctors and pharmacists usually speak to patients with the polite honorific ending -(으)세요, so the questions you hear may look a little different from the forms you use yourself.

  • 어디가 아프세요?{{say:어디가 아프세요?}} Where does it hurt? (Also 어디가 불편하세요?{{say:어디가 불편하세요?}}, literally "where is it uncomfortable?")
  • 언제부터 아프셨어요?{{say:언제부터 아프셨어요?}} Since when has it hurt?
  • 알레르기 있으세요?{{say:알레르기 있으세요?}} Do you have any allergies?

Our questions guide covers 어디, 언제 and the other question words you'll hear.

Putting it together

약사: 어디가 아프세요?{{say:어디가 아프세요?}}
Pharmacist: What seems to be the problem?

손님: 목이 아프고 열이 나요.{{say:목이 아프고 열이 나요}}
Customer: I have a sore throat and a fever.

약사: 이 약을 하루에 세 번 드세요.{{say:이 약을 하루에 세 번 드세요}}
Pharmacist: Take this medicine three times a day.

손님: 감사합니다.{{say:감사합니다}}
Customer: Thank you.

The ending -고 in 아프고 links two sentences, like "and". See all guides for more.
