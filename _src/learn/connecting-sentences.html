From short sentences to longer ones

So far, our guides have mostly used short sentences: 배고파요. 밥 먹어요. To sound more natural, you'll want to join ideas together: "I'm hungry, so I'm eating", "it's expensive but tasty". In Korean, this is done with endings attached to the first verb, called connective endings. This guide covers the three most useful ones, plus some words for linking separate sentences.

A key point: in Korean, only the last verb in the sentence carries the polite -요, and often it's the only one that carries the tense too. The verbs before it take a connective ending instead.

-고: "and"

-고 links two actions or two descriptions, like "and". Just add it to the verb stem; there's no 아/어 rule:

  • 이 식당은 싸고 맛있어요.{{say:이 식당은 싸고 맛있어요}} This restaurant is cheap and tasty.
  • 저는 커피를 마시고 친구는 차를 마셔요.{{say:저는 커피를 마시고 친구는 차를 마셔요}} I drink coffee, and my friend drinks tea.
  • 밥을 먹고 영화를 봤어요.{{say:밥을 먹고 영화를 봤어요}} I ate and then watched a movie.

In the last example, only 봤어요 is in the past tense, but both actions are understood as past. You've seen -고 before in 목이 아프고 열이 나요 in our pharmacy guide.

-지만: "but"

-지만 means "but" or "although". It also attaches directly to the stem:

  • 한국어는 어렵지만 재미있어요.{{say:한국어는 어렵지만 재미있어요}} Korean is hard, but it's fun.
  • 비싸지만 맛있어요.{{say:비싸지만 맛있어요}} It's expensive, but it's tasty.
  • 어제는 추웠지만 오늘은 따뜻해요.{{say:어제는 추웠지만 오늘은 따뜻해요}} Yesterday was cold, but today is warm.

-지만 takes the past tense on its own verb whenever that part is in the past, as in 추웠지만, or 매웠지만 in the dialogue below. -고 can too (어제는 추웠고 오늘은 따뜻해요); only when -고 means "and then" does the first verb skip the tense. With nouns, use 이지만 after a consonant and 지만 after a vowel (의사지만; 의사이지만 is also correct): 학생이지만 일도 해요{{say:학생이지만 일도 해요}} ("I'm a student, but I also work").

-아서/-어서: "so" and "and then"

-아서/-어서 follows the same 아/어 rule as the present tense: take the polite present form and replace -요 with -서. 가요 → 가서, 먹어요 → 먹어서, 해요 → 해서. (With 이다, it's 이어서 or 여서: 학생이어서, 의사여서.) It has two main uses.

1. Reasons: "so", "because"

  • 배고파서 밥을 먹었어요.{{say:배고파서 밥을 먹었어요}} I was hungry, so I ate.
  • 비가 와서 집에 있어요.{{say:비가 와서 집에 있어요}} It's raining, so I'm staying home.
  • 늦어서 죄송해요.{{say:늦어서 죄송해요}} Sorry I'm late. (literally "I'm late, so I'm sorry")

Note that the reason part doesn't take the past tense even when the story is in the past: 배고파서, not 배고팠어서. Also, -아서/-어서 isn't used before a suggestion or a request. For "it's raining, so let's stay home", you'd use a different ending, which you'll meet later in your studies.

2. Sequence: "and then (there)"

When the second action depends on the first, such as going somewhere and then doing something there, -아서/-어서 means "and then":

  • 시장에 가서 과일을 샀어요.{{say:시장에 가서 과일을 샀어요}} I went to the market and bought fruit (there).
  • 친구를 만나서 커피를 마셨어요.{{say:친구를 만나서 커피를 마셨어요}} I met a friend and had coffee (with them).

Compare 시장에 가고 과일을 샀어요, which sounds odd: -고 just lists the two actions and doesn't say the fruit was bought there. With 가서, it was.

Linking separate sentences

You can also keep two short sentences and join them with a linking word at the start of the second:

KoreanMeaningExample
그리고{{say:그리고}}and, also밥을 먹었어요. 그리고 산책했어요.
I ate. And then I took a walk.
그런데{{say:그런데}}but, by the way맛있어요. 그런데 좀 매워요.
It's tasty. But it's a bit spicy.
그래서{{say:그래서}}so, therefore피곤했어요. 그래서 일찍 잤어요.
I was tired. So I went to bed early.
그래도{{say:그래도}}even so, still비가 와요. 그래도 갈 거예요.
It's raining. I'm still going to go.

그런데 is often shortened to 근데{{say:근데}} in speech, and it's also used to change the subject, like "by the way". These words are a good starting point: they're easy to use, and they make even simple sentences flow.

Putting it together

가: 어제 뭐 했어요?{{say:어제 뭐 했어요?}}
A: What did you do yesterday?

나: 친구를 만나서 명동에 갔어요. 쇼핑하고 떡볶이를 먹었어요.{{say:친구를 만나서 명동에 갔어요. 쇼핑하고 떡볶이를 먹었어요}}
B: I met a friend and we went to Myeong-dong. We went shopping and ate tteokbokki.

가: 떡볶이 맛있었어요?{{say:떡볶이 맛있었어요?}}
A: Was the tteokbokki good?

나: 너무 매웠지만 맛있었어요. 그래서 또 갈 거예요!{{say:너무 매웠지만 맛있었어요. 그래서 또 갈 거예요}}
B: It was really spicy, but it was delicious. So I'm going to go again!
