You're invited!

Being invited to someone's home is a sign of real friendship in Korea, and a great chance to use your Korean. Hosts are usually very generous, and a few polite phrases from you will make a big impression. This guide follows a visit from the invitation to saying goodbye. Customs vary from family to family, so take these as common habits rather than strict rules.

The invitation

  • 주말에 우리 집에 놀러 오세요.{{say:주말에 우리 집에 놀러 오세요}} Come over to our place this weekend.
  • 초대해 주셔서 감사합니다.{{say:초대해 주셔서 감사합니다}} Thank you for inviting me.
  • 뭐 가져갈까요?{{say:뭐 가져갈까요?}} Shall I bring anything?

Koreans say 우리 집 ("our house") even when they live alone; it's the normal way to say "my home". 놀러 오다 means "to come over to hang out", from 놀다 ("to play", "to have fun").

Bringing a gift

It's polite not to arrive empty-handed. Fruit, drinks, cakes or snacks are always welcome. When someone has moved into a new home, friends are often invited to a housewarming party called a 집들이{{say:집들이}}. Traditional housewarming gifts include rolls of toilet paper, 휴지, wishing that everything goes smoothly, like paper unrolling, and laundry detergent, 세제, wishing that good fortune bubbles up like suds.

When you hand over a gift, use both hands, and say something modest:

  • 별거 아니에요.{{say:별거 아니에요}} It's nothing much.
  • 작은 선물이에요.{{say:작은 선물이에요}} It's a small gift.

The host may answer 뭘 이런 걸 다 사 오셨어요{{say:뭘 이런 걸 다 사 오셨어요}}, something like "oh, you shouldn't have!" It sounds like a complaint, but it's a warm thank-you.

At the door

As you step in, say 실례하겠습니다{{say:실례하겠습니다}}, literally "I'm going to be rude (by coming in)", a polite way to announce yourself. The host will say 들어오세요{{say:들어오세요}} ("come in") or 어서 오세요{{say:어서 오세요}} ("welcome").

Take your shoes off at the entryway, 현관{{say:현관}}, before stepping up onto the floor. This is a firm custom in Korean homes. Because many homes have heated floors, as our hotel guide describes, people often sit on the floor, sometimes on a cushion called a 방석{{say:방석}}. Clean socks are a good idea!

At the table

Korean hosts often play down the meal they've prepared, with phrases like this:

  • 차린 건 없지만 많이 드세요.{{say:차린 건 없지만 많이 드세요}} There's not much, but please eat plenty.
  • 편하게 드세요.{{say:편하게 드세요}} Please make yourself at home and eat.
  • 더 드세요.{{say:더 드세요}} Have some more.

Before you start, say 잘 먹겠습니다, and when you've finished, 잘 먹었습니다, as in our greetings guide. If older people are at the table, it's polite to wait until they start eating before you do. When an older person pours you a drink, hold your glass with both hands.

If you're offered more food than you can eat, a polite way to say no is 배불러서 더 못 먹겠어요{{say:배불러서 더 못 먹겠어요}} ("I'm so full I couldn't eat any more"). Our taste and food words guide has more words for talking about the meal.

Compliments

  • 집이 정말 예뻐요.{{say:집이 정말 예뻐요}} Your home is really lovely.
  • 음식이 정말 맛있어요.{{say:음식이 정말 맛있어요}} The food is really delicious.
  • 이거 직접 만드셨어요?{{say:이거 직접 만드셨어요?}} Did you make this yourself?

Expect a modest reply such as 아니에요 ("not at all"), even when your host is clearly pleased.

Saying goodbye

  • 이제 가 볼게요.{{say:이제 가 볼게요}} I'd better be going now.
  • 오늘 정말 감사했어요.{{say:오늘 정말 감사했어요}} Thank you so much for today.
  • 다음에는 제가 대접할게요.{{say:다음에는 제가 대접할게요}} Next time, it's my treat.

The host will say 조심히 가세요{{say:조심히 가세요}} ("get home safely") or 안녕히 가세요. Hosts often walk guests to the door or even outside. When they do, you can say 들어가세요{{say:들어가세요}} ("please go back in"), the same phrase that ends many phone calls in our phone calls guide.

Putting it together

손님: 안녕하세요! 실례하겠습니다.{{say:안녕하세요! 실례하겠습니다}}
Guest: Hello! Excuse me for coming in.

주인: 어서 오세요. 들어오세요.{{say:어서 오세요. 들어오세요}}
Host: Welcome. Come in.

손님: 이거 별거 아니에요. 과일 좀 사 왔어요.{{say:이거 별거 아니에요. 과일 좀 사 왔어요}}
Guest: This is nothing much. I brought some fruit.

주인: 뭘 이런 걸 다 사 오셨어요. 감사해요. 편하게 앉으세요.{{say:뭘 이런 걸 다 사 오셨어요. 감사해요. 편하게 앉으세요}}
Host: Oh, you shouldn't have. Thank you. Please make yourself comfortable.

Don't worry about getting every custom right: hosts know you're learning, and your effort to speak Korean will be appreciated. See all guides for more.
