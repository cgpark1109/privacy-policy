Two big family holidays

Korea's two biggest holidays, 설날{{say:설날}} (Lunar New Year) and 추석{{say:추석}} (the autumn harvest festival), both follow the lunar calendar, so their dates change every year. Each lasts three days: the day itself, plus the day before and the day after. Millions of people travel to their hometowns to spend the holiday with family, so trains and highways are packed, and cities like Seoul become unusually quiet. They're the two great 명절{{say:명절}}, or "traditional holidays".

Seollal: Lunar New Year

설날 is the first day of the first month of the lunar calendar, usually in late January or February. Many Koreans celebrate both January 1 and 설날, but 설날 is the bigger family holiday.

  • 떡국{{say:떡국}}: a soup with thin slices of rice cake, eaten on the morning of 설날. There's a saying that eating a bowl of 떡국 makes you a year older (in the traditional way of counting age).
  • 세배{{say:세배}}: a deep, formal bow that younger family members make to their elders, wishing them a happy new year. Elders reply with good wishes and often give children money, 세뱃돈{{say:세뱃돈}}.
  • 한복{{say:한복}}: traditional clothes, which some families still wear for the holiday.
  • Games: families often play 윷놀이{{say:윷놀이}}, a board game played by throwing four sticks.

Chuseok: the harvest festival

추석 falls on the fifteenth day of the eighth lunar month, at the full moon, usually in September or early October. It's often compared to Thanksgiving, as a time to give thanks for the harvest.

  • 송편{{say:송편}}: half-moon-shaped rice cakes filled with sesame, beans or chestnuts, steamed on pine needles. Families often make them together.
  • 차례{{say:차례}}: a memorial ceremony for ancestors, held on the morning of the holiday with a table of carefully arranged food. It's also held at 설날, though many families now keep it simple or skip it.
  • 성묘{{say:성묘}}: visiting ancestors' graves to pay respects, traditionally on the day itself after 차례, though many families now go a few days early to avoid the traffic. Tidying the graves, 벌초{{say:벌초}}, is usually done in the weeks before 추석.
  • The full moon: people look at the bright harvest moon and make a wish.

October 9, 한글날, celebrates the Korean alphabet, the same one you're learning; our How Hangul Works guide tells its story. Many holiday names end in -절, from a Chinese character meaning "festival" or "special day", or in -날, the native Korean word for "day". For saying dates like these, see our days and dates guide.

When a holiday falls on a weekend or on another holiday, a substitute holiday, 대체 공휴일{{say:대체 공휴일}}, is usually given on the next weekday. This applies to most, but not all, holidays. Election days are public holidays too.

Putting it together

가: 이번 추석에 고향에 가세요?{{say:이번 추석에 고향에 가세요?}}
A: Are you going to your hometown this Chuseok?

나: 네, 부산에 가요. 가족들하고 송편을 만들 거예요.{{say:네, 부산에 가요. 가족들하고 송편을 만들 거예요}}
B: Yes, I'm going to Busan. I'm going to make songpyeon with my family.

가: 좋겠네요! 추석 잘 보내세요.{{say:좋겠네요! 추석 잘 보내세요}}
A: That sounds lovely! Have a good Chuseok.

나: 감사합니다. 즐거운 명절 보내세요!{{say:감사합니다. 즐거운 명절 보내세요}}
B: Thank you. Happy holidays to you too!
