Let's meet up

Making plans is where Korean starts to feel like real conversation: you need to talk about time, say what you'd like to do, make suggestions and respond to them. This guide brings together several things from earlier guides, especially the numbers guide for telling the time and the present tense guide for verbs, and adds two very useful new endings.

Telling the time

As our numbers guide explains, Korean uses native numbers for hours and Sino-Korean numbers for minutes:

TimeKorean
1:00한 시{{say:한 시}}
3:15세 시 십오 분{{say:세 시 십오 분}}
7:30일곱 시 반{{say:일곱 시 반}} (반 = half)
12:00열두 시{{say:열두 시}}

Korean doesn't usually say "a.m." and "p.m." at the end. Instead, you put a word for the part of the day in front:

  • 오전{{say:오전}} (morning, a.m.) and 오후{{say:오후}} (afternoon, p.m.): 오후 두 시 is 2 p.m.
  • 아침{{say:아침}} (morning), 점심{{say:점심}} (midday, lunch), 저녁{{say:저녁}} (evening, dinner) and 밤{{say:밤}} (night): 저녁 일곱 시 is 7 in the evening.

To say "at" a time, add 에: 세 시에 만나요{{say:세 시에 만나요}} ("let's meet at three"). To say "about", add 쯤: 세 시쯤{{say:세 시쯤}} ("around three"). For days of the week, see our days and dates guide.

Saying what you want to do: -고 싶어요

To say "I want to …", take the verb stem and add -고 싶어요. There's no 아/어 rule to worry about: just add it.

  • 영화 보고 싶어요.{{say:영화 보고 싶어요}} I want to see a movie. (보다)
  • 떡볶이 먹고 싶어요.{{say:떡볶이 먹고 싶어요}} I want to eat tteokbokki. (먹다)
  • 한강에 가고 싶어요.{{say:한강에 가고 싶어요}} I want to go to the Han River. (가다)
  • 뭐 하고 싶어요?{{say:뭐 하고 싶어요?}} What do you want to do?

Making a suggestion: -(으)ㄹ까요?

To suggest doing something together ("shall we …?"), add -ㄹ까요? to a stem ending in a vowel, and -을까요? to a stem ending in a consonant:

VerbSuggestionMeaning
만나다만날까요?{{say:만날까요?}}Shall we meet?
가다같이 갈까요?{{say:같이 갈까요?}}Shall we go together?
먹다뭐 먹을까요?{{say:뭐 먹을까요?}}What shall we eat?
하다뭐 할까요?{{say:뭐 할까요?}}What shall we do?

같이{{say:같이}} ("together") goes very well with this ending. It's spelled 같이 but pronounced [가치].

The polite present tense can also be a suggestion, depending on your tone: 같이 가요!{{say:같이 가요}} means "let's go together!".

Saying yes

  • 좋아요!{{say:좋아요}} Sounds good!
  • 그래요.{{say:그래요}} OK, let's do that.
  • 몇 시에 만날까요?{{say:몇 시에 만날까요?}} What time shall we meet?
  • 어디에서 만날까요?{{say:어디에서 만날까요?}} Where shall we meet?

Saying no, politely

Koreans tend to decline indirectly, softening the "no" with an apology or a reason:

  • 미안해요, 그날은 좀 바빠요.{{say:미안해요, 그날은 좀 바빠요}} Sorry, I'm a bit busy that day.
  • 다음에 같이 가요.{{say:다음에 같이 가요}} Let's go together next time.
  • 토요일은 어때요?{{say:토요일은 어때요?}} How about Saturday?

어때요? ("how is it?") is a handy way to suggest an alternative: put the new time, day or place in front, followed by 은/는.

Setting the time and place

Korean friends often meet at a well-known spot, like a subway exit: 강남역 11번 출구에서 만나요{{say:강남역 십일 번 출구에서 만나요}} ("let's meet at Gangnam Station, Exit 11"). Our getting around guide has more station words. The word for an appointment or a plan with someone is 약속{{say:약속}}: 약속 있어요 means "I have plans".

Putting it together

가: 이번 주말에 시간 있어요?{{say:이번 주말에 시간 있어요?}}
A: Are you free this weekend?

나: 토요일은 약속 있어요. 일요일은 괜찮아요.{{say:토요일은 약속 있어요. 일요일은 괜찮아요}}
B: I have plans on Saturday. Sunday is fine.

가: 그럼 일요일에 같이 영화 볼까요?{{say:그럼 일요일에 같이 영화 볼까요?}}
A: Then shall we see a movie together on Sunday?

나: 좋아요! 몇 시에 만날까요?{{say:좋아요! 몇 시에 만날까요?}}
B: Sounds good! What time shall we meet?

가: 오후 두 시쯤 어때요? 강남역 11번 출구에서 만나요.{{say:오후 두 시쯤 어때요? 강남역 십일 번 출구에서 만나요}}
A: How about around 2 p.m.? Let's meet at Gangnam Station, Exit 11.
