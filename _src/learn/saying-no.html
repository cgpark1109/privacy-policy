Two little words for "not"

English makes negatives with "not", often with a helper like "don't", "doesn't" or "didn't" that changes with the subject and tense. Korean is simpler: in most sentences, you put one short word in front of the verb. The two words are 안{{say:안}} ("not") and 못{{say:못}} ("can't"), and they work with every tense you've learned so far in our present tense, past tense and future tense guides.

안: "not" or "don't"

Put 안 directly in front of the verb or adjective:

PositiveNegativeMeaning
가요안 가요{{say:안 가요}}I don't go. / I'm not going.
먹어요안 먹어요{{say:안 먹어요}}I don't eat it.
비싸요안 비싸요{{say:안 비싸요}}It's not expensive.
매워요안 매워요{{say:안 매워요}}It's not spicy.
갔어요안 갔어요{{say:안 갔어요}}I didn't go.

안 usually means you choose not to do something, or that something simply isn't the case: 저는 고기를 안 먹어요{{say:저는 고기를 안 먹어요}} ("I don't eat meat").

Verbs made with 하다

Many verbs are a noun plus 하다, like 공부하다 (to study) or 운동하다 (to exercise). With these, 안 goes between the noun and 하다:

  • 공부 안 해요.{{say:공부 안 해요}} I don't study. (not 안 공부해요)
  • 오늘은 운동 안 했어요.{{say:오늘은 운동 안 했어요}} I didn't exercise today.

This only applies when the part before 하다 is a noun you can use on its own. Adjectives like 깨끗하다 (clean) and verbs like 좋아하다 (to like) take 안 in front: 안 깨끗해요{{say:안 깨끗해요}}, 안 좋아해요{{say:안 좋아해요}}.

못: "can't"

못 goes in the same place as 안, but it means you can't do something, or couldn't because of circumstances, not because you didn't want to:

  • 매운 음식은 못 먹어요.{{say:매운 음식은 못 먹어요}} I can't eat spicy food.
  • 내일은 바빠서 못 가요.{{say:내일은 바빠서 못 가요}} I'm busy tomorrow, so I can't go.
  • 어제 잠을 못 잤어요.{{say:어제 잠을 못 잤어요}} I couldn't sleep last night.

Compare 안 갔어요 ("I didn't go") with 못 갔어요 ("I couldn't go"). When you turn down an invitation, 못 sounds kinder, because it suggests that you'd like to come but can't. With 하다 verbs made from a noun, 못 also goes in the middle: 운동 못 했어요{{say:운동 못 했어요}} ("I couldn't exercise").

못 changes the sound of the next syllable: 못 가요 sounds like [모까요], because the ㄱ after it becomes tense, and 못 먹어요 sounds like [몬머거요], because the t sound turns nasal before ㅁ, as explained in our batchim guide. 못 is used with verbs, not adjectives: to say "it's not expensive", always use 안.

잘 못해요: "I'm not good at it"

못하다, written as one word, means "to be bad at" (it's also the 못해요 in -지 못해요, below). With 잘 ("well"), it's a modest way to talk about your skills:

  • 한국어를 잘 못해요.{{say:한국어를 잘 못해요}} My Korean isn't very good.
  • 노래를 잘 못해요.{{say:노래를 잘 못해요}} I'm not a good singer.

Watch the spacing: 잘못해요, written together, means "to make a mistake" or "to do something wrong", as in 제가 잘못했어요 ("it was my fault").

The long forms: -지 않아요 and -지 못해요

There's a second way to make negatives: add -지 않아요 (for 안) or -지 못해요 (for 못) to the verb stem. There's no 아/어 rule to worry about:

Short formLong formMeaning
안 가요가지 않아요{{say:가지 않아요}}I don't go.
안 비싸요비싸지 않아요{{say:비싸지 않아요}}It's not expensive.
안 갔어요가지 않았어요{{say:가지 않았어요}}I didn't go.
못 가요가지 못해요{{say:가지 못해요}}I can't go.

The meaning is the same. In everyday conversation the short forms are more common, while the long forms sound a little more careful and are frequent in writing, news and formal speech. The long form is also handy with long verbs, especially 하다 verbs: 공부하지 않아요{{say:공부하지 않아요}} is just as correct as 공부 안 해요. Notice that the tense goes on the last part only: 가지 않았어요.

Three special negatives

A few common words have their own negative partner instead of using 안:

PositiveNegativeExample
이에요/예요 (to be)아니에요{{say:아니에요}}저는 학생이 아니에요.{{say:저는 학생이 아니에요}} I'm not a student.
있어요 (there is, I have)없어요{{say:없어요}}시간이 없어요.{{say:시간이 없어요}} I don't have time.
알아요 (I know)몰라요{{say:몰라요}}잘 몰라요.{{say:잘 몰라요}} I don't really know.

With 아니에요, the noun takes 이/가: 학생이 아니에요, 의사가 아니에요. Our particles guide explains how to choose between them. On its own, 아니에요 is also a polite way to say "no" or "not at all", for example after someone thanks you, as in our greetings guide, or praises you, as in our introductions guide.

Making it softer or stronger

Korean speakers often add a word to show how strong the "no" is:

  • 별로 안 좋아해요.{{say:별로 안 좋아해요}} I don't really like it. (별로: not particularly)
  • 전혀 안 매워요.{{say:전혀 안 매워요}} It's not spicy at all. (전혀: not at all)
  • 아직 안 먹었어요.{{say:아직 안 먹었어요}} I haven't eaten yet. (아직: yet, still)
  • 아무것도 안 했어요.{{say:아무것도 안 했어요}} I didn't do anything. (아무것도: anything, nothing)

별로 and 전혀 are normally used with negatives. One common exception is a short 별로예요{{say:별로예요}}, which means "it's not great", a polite way to say you don't think much of something.

"Don't": -지 마세요

To ask someone not to do something, add -지 마세요 to the verb stem. You'll see it on signs everywhere, along with the more formal -지 마시오:

  • 들어가지 마세요.{{say:들어가지 마세요}} Do not enter.
  • 만지지 마세요.{{say:만지지 마세요}} Please don't touch.
  • 사진 찍지 마세요.{{say:사진 찍지 마세요}} No photos, please.
  • 걱정하지 마세요.{{say:걱정하지 마세요}} Don't worry.

Another phrase you'll hear a lot is 안 돼요{{say:안 돼요}}: "you can't", "it's not allowed" or "it doesn't work". A shop might say 카드 안 돼요 ("we don't take cards"), and you might say 와이파이가 안 돼요 ("the Wi-Fi isn't working").

Answering negative questions

Here Korean and English work differently. In Korean, 네 means "what you said is right", and 아니요 means "what you said is wrong". So when someone asks a negative question, the answer can look backwards to an English speaker:

가: 안 매워요?{{say:안 매워요?}}
A: Isn't it spicy?

나: 네, 안 매워요.{{say:네, 안 매워요}}
B: No, it isn't. (literally "Yes, it's not spicy.")

나: 아니요, 매워요.{{say:아니요, 매워요}}
B: Yes, it is. (literally "No, it's spicy.")

If this feels confusing, just answer with the full verb, 안 매워요 or 매워요, and you'll always be understood.

Putting it together

가: 오늘 저녁에 같이 밥 먹을까요?{{say:오늘 저녁에 같이 밥 먹을까요?}}
A: Shall we have dinner together this evening?

나: 미안해요, 오늘은 일이 많아서 못 가요.{{say:미안해요, 오늘은 일이 많아서 못 가요}}
B: Sorry, I have a lot of work today, so I can't make it.

가: 괜찮아요. 그럼 내일은 어때요?{{say:괜찮아요. 그럼 내일은 어때요?}}
A: That's OK. How about tomorrow, then?

나: 내일은 약속 없어요. 좋아요!{{say:내일은 약속 없어요. 좋아요}}
B: I don't have any plans tomorrow. Sounds good!
