Three kinds of "can"

English uses "can" for several different things: "I can come tomorrow" (it's possible), "I can swim" (I know how), and "can I sit here?" (asking permission). Korean usually uses a different pattern for each. This guide covers all three, and shows how they fit with 못 from our guide to saying no.

-(으)ㄹ 수 있어요: "can", "be able to"

The basic pattern is -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요. Literally it means something like "there is a way to …": 수 means "a way" or "a means", and 있어요 means "there is". It's built like the future ending in our future tense guide:

  • If the stem ends in a vowel, add -ㄹ 수 있어요.
  • If it ends in any other consonant, add -을 수 있어요.
  • If it ends in ㄹ, just add 수 있어요.
VerbCanMeaning
가다갈 수 있어요{{say:갈 수 있어요}}I can go.
하다할 수 있어요{{say:할 수 있어요}}I can do it.
먹다먹을 수 있어요{{say:먹을 수 있어요}}I can eat it.
읽다읽을 수 있어요{{say:읽을 수 있어요}}I can read it.
만들다만들 수 있어요{{say:만들 수 있어요}}I can make it.

As with the future ending, the 수 after ㄹ sounds tense: 갈 수 있어요 is pronounced [갈 쑤 이써요]. A few verbs change their stem, just as they do in the present tense: 듣다 (to listen) becomes 들을 수 있어요, and 돕다 (to help) becomes 도울 수 있어요.

For "can't", replace 있어요 with 없어요:

  • 내일은 갈 수 없어요.{{say:내일은 갈 수 없어요}} I can't go tomorrow.
  • 너무 매워서 먹을 수 없어요.{{say:너무 매워서 먹을 수 없어요}} It's too spicy, so I can't eat it.

For the past, put the tense on the last verb: 갈 수 있었어요{{say:갈 수 있었어요}} ("I was able to go"), 갈 수 없었어요{{say:갈 수 없었어요}} ("I couldn't go").

수 없어요 or 못?

갈 수 없어요 and 못 가요 mean much the same thing: "I can't go". In everyday conversation, 못 is shorter and more common. -(으)ㄹ 수 없어요 sounds a little more formal or emphatic, and it's often used for rules and things that are impossible for anyone: 여기에서는 담배를 피울 수 없어요{{say:여기에서는 담배를 피울 수 없어요}} ("you can't smoke here").

For "can", though, there's no short form: -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요 is the everyday way to say it.

-(으)ㄹ 줄 알아요: knowing how

For skills you've learned, like swimming, driving or speaking a language, Korean often uses -(으)ㄹ 줄 알아요, literally "I know the way to …". The negative is -(으)ㄹ 줄 몰라요, with 몰라요 ("I don't know"):

  • 수영할 줄 알아요?{{say:수영할 줄 알아요?}} Can you swim? (Do you know how to swim?)
  • 운전할 줄 몰라요.{{say:운전할 줄 몰라요}} I can't drive. (I don't know how to drive.)
  • 김치찌개 만들 줄 알아요.{{say:김치찌개 만들 줄 알아요}} I know how to make kimchi stew.

You've met this pattern in 영어 할 줄 알아요? ("can you speak English?") in our questions guide. -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요 is also fine for skills: 수영할 수 있어요 is correct too. To say how well you do something, use 잘해요 ("I'm good at it") or 잘 못해요 ("I'm not good at it").

Asking permission: -아도/어도 돼요?

To ask "can I …?" or "may I …?", Korean usually uses -아도/어도 돼요?, literally "is it OK even if I …?". It follows the same 아/어 rule as the present tense from our verbs guide: take the polite present form and replace -요 with -도 돼요.

PresentAsking permissionMeaning
앉아요여기 앉아도 돼요?{{say:여기 앉아도 돼요?}}May I sit here?
찍어요사진 찍어도 돼요?{{say:사진 찍어도 돼요?}}Can I take a picture?
들어가요들어가도 돼요?{{say:들어가도 돼요?}}May I come in?
해요카드로 계산해도 돼요?{{say:카드로 계산해도 돼요?}}Can I pay by card?

To say yes, repeat the verb: 네, 앉아도 돼요{{say:네, 앉아도 돼요}}, or simply 네, 그럼요{{say:네, 그럼요}} ("yes, of course"). To say no, you'll often hear 안 돼요{{say:안 돼요}} ("you can't"), or the longer -(으)면 안 돼요, "it's not OK if you …": 여기서 사진 찍으면 안 돼요{{say:여기서 사진 찍으면 안 돼요}} ("you can't take pictures here").

-(으)ㄹ 수 있어요? can also ask whether something is possible, as in 짐 맡길 수 있어요? ("can I leave my luggage?") in our hotel guide. For simply asking if something is allowed, -아도/어도 돼요? is the more natural choice.

Asking for help: 도와줄 수 있어요?

-(으)ㄹ 수 있어요? is also a polite way to ask someone to do something for you:

  • 좀 도와주실 수 있어요?{{say:좀 도와주실 수 있어요?}} Could you help me, please?
  • 천천히 말해 줄 수 있어요?{{say:천천히 말해 줄 수 있어요?}} Could you speak slowly?

The first example adds the honorific -시- (도와주실), which makes it extra polite, a good choice with strangers. Our politeness guide explains more about it.

Putting it together

가: 이번 주말에 같이 수영하러 갈 수 있어요?{{say:이번 주말에 같이 수영하러 갈 수 있어요?}}
A: Can you go swimming with me this weekend?

나: 가고 싶어요. 그런데 저는 수영할 줄 몰라요.{{say:가고 싶어요. 그런데 저는 수영할 줄 몰라요}}
B: I'd like to. But I don't know how to swim.

가: 괜찮아요. 제가 가르쳐 줄 수 있어요.{{say:괜찮아요. 제가 가르쳐 줄 수 있어요}}
A: That's OK. I can teach you.

나: 정말요? 그럼 친구도 같이 가도 돼요?{{say:정말요? 그럼 친구도 같이 가도 돼요?}}
B: Really? Then can my friend come too?

가: 그럼요!{{say:그럼요}}
A: Of course!

-(으)러 가요, as in 수영하러 갈 수 있어요? above, means "go (somewhere) to do something". For more ways to make plans, see our making plans guide, or see all guides.
