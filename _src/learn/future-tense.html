Present, past and now the future

With the present tense from our verbs guide and the past tense from our past tense guide, you can already talk about what you do and what you did. This guide adds the future: what you're going to do, and what you think will happen. The main ending is -(으)ㄹ 거예요, and it's one of the easiest endings in Korean, because it has no 아/어 rule.

Making -(으)ㄹ 거예요

Take the verb stem, then:

  • If the stem ends in a vowel, add -ㄹ 거예요.
  • If it ends in any other consonant, add -을 거예요.
  • If it ends in ㄹ, just add 거예요.
VerbFutureMeaning
가다갈 거예요{{say:갈 거예요}}I'm going to go.
보다볼 거예요{{say:볼 거예요}}I'm going to see.
하다할 거예요{{say:할 거예요}}I'm going to do.
먹다먹을 거예요{{say:먹을 거예요}}I'm going to eat.
읽다읽을 거예요{{say:읽을 거예요}}I'm going to read.
살다살 거예요{{say:살 거예요}}I'm going to live.

In 갈 거예요, the ㄹ sits under the last syllable of the stem as a final consonant: 가 + ㄹ = 갈. When you say it, the 거 after ㄹ sounds tense, like [갈 꺼예요], but it's always written 거. Our plain, aspirated and tense consonants guide explains what a tense sound is; this tensing after the -ㄹ ending is a separate rule you'll pick up by listening.

Plans and intentions

The most common use is for things you plan or intend to do:

  • 내일 친구를 만날 거예요.{{say:내일 친구를 만날 거예요}} I'm going to meet a friend tomorrow.
  • 다음 달에 한국에 갈 거예요.{{say:다음 달에 한국에 갈 거예요}} I'm going to Korea next month.
  • 주말에 뭐 할 거예요?{{say:주말에 뭐 할 거예요?}} What are you going to do this weekend?

Predictions and guesses

-(으)ㄹ 거예요 is also used for what you think will happen, or what's probably true:

  • 내일은 추울 거예요.{{say:내일은 추울 거예요}} It'll probably be cold tomorrow. (춥다: the ㅂ becomes 우, as in 추워요.)
  • 아마 괜찮을 거예요.{{say:아마 괜찮을 거예요}} It'll probably be fine. (아마: probably)
  • 민지 씨는 지금 집에 있을 거예요.{{say:민지 씨는 지금 집에 있을 거예요}} Minji is probably at home now.

The last example shows that the "future" ending isn't always about the future: it can also be a guess about now.

-(으)ㄹ게요: "I'll do it" (a promise to the listener)

You'll often hear a similar-looking ending, -(으)ㄹ게요. It's used when you decide to do something, usually in response to the other person, a bit like "I'll …" in English:

  • 제가 할게요.{{say:제가 할게요}} I'll do it.
  • 이따가 전화할게요.{{say:이따가 전화할게요}} I'll call you later.
  • 먼저 갈게요.{{say:먼저 갈게요}} I'll head off first. (said when leaving before others)

The difference in a nutshell: 갈 거예요 tells someone your plan ("I'm going to go"), while 갈게요 responds to them with a decision or promise ("OK, I'll go"). -(으)ㄹ게요 is only used for your own actions, and it can't be used to ask a question: to ask about someone's plans, use 갈 거예요? It's written 게 but sounds like [께], just as 거 sounds like [꺼]. You've met it already in the restaurant guide: 먹고 갈게요 ("I'll eat here").

The present tense for the near future

Just like English "I'm leaving tomorrow", Korean often uses the polite present tense for plans that are already fixed, especially with a time word:

  • 내일 부산에 가요.{{say:내일 부산에 가요}} I'm going to Busan tomorrow.
  • 수업은 세 시에 시작해요.{{say:수업은 세 시에 시작해요}} The class starts at three.

Saying you won't

Put 안 in front, as with the other tenses: 안 갈 거예요{{say:안 갈 거예요}} ("I'm not going to go"). With 하다 verbs made from a noun, 안 goes just before 할 거예요: 운동 안 할 거예요{{say:운동 안 할 거예요}} ("I'm not going to work out").

Putting it together

가: 겨울방학에 뭐 할 거예요?{{say:겨울방학에 뭐 할 거예요?}}
A: What are you going to do over the winter vacation?

나: 제주도에 갈 거예요. 가족하고 같이 여행할 거예요.{{say:제주도에 갈 거예요. 가족하고 같이 여행할 거예요}}
B: I'm going to Jeju Island. I'm going to travel with my family.

가: 좋겠네요! 제주도는 서울보다 따뜻할 거예요.{{say:좋겠네요! 제주도는 서울보다 따뜻할 거예요}}
A: That sounds great! It'll probably be warmer than Seoul.

나: 사진 많이 찍어서 보여 줄게요.{{say:사진 많이 찍어서 보여 줄게요}}
B: I'll take lots of photos and show you.
