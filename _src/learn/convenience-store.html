The 편의점

Convenience stores, 편의점{{say:편의점}}, are on almost every street corner in Korea, and many are open 24 hours. They sell far more than snacks: hot meals, coffee, transport cards, phone chargers and everyday medicines. Many have a small table where you can eat. It's one of the easiest places to practice Korean, because the conversation at the counter follows the same few steps every time.

Look out for signs like 1+1 (read 원 플러스 원{{say:원 플러스 원}}) and 2+1: buy one and get one free, or buy two and get a third free. These deals are called 행사{{say:행사}} ("promotion"), and the clerk may remind you: 1+1이에요. 하나 더 가져오세요 ("it's buy one, get one free; grab another one").

Hot food and hot water

Most convenience stores have a microwave, 전자레인지{{say:전자레인지}}, and a hot water dispenser for cup noodles. In many stores you heat your own food after paying, but some clerks will ask:

  • 데워 드릴까요?{{say:데워 드릴까요?}} Shall I heat it up for you?
  • 네, 데워 주세요.{{say:네, 데워 주세요}} Yes, please heat it up.
  • 전자레인지 어디 있어요?{{say:전자레인지 어디 있어요?}} Where's the microwave?
  • 젓가락 주세요.{{say:젓가락 주세요}} Chopsticks, please. (spoon: 숟가락)

데워 주세요 is the request pattern from our guide to asking someone to do something, from 데우다 ("to heat up").

At the counter

When you pay, the clerk will usually scan your items and ask a few quick questions. These are the ones to listen for:

You'll hearMeaningYou can say

카드로 하시겠어요?{{say:카드로 하시겠어요?}}Will you pay by card?네, 카드로 할게요 / 현금으로 할게요

Bags usually cost a little extra, which is why the clerk asks. 괜찮아요, as our guide to saying no explains, is a polite "no, thanks". Card payment is accepted almost everywhere, and you can also top up your transport card here: 교통카드 충전해 주세요, as in our getting around guide.

More than snacks

Many convenience stores also have an ATM, sell phone chargers, umbrellas and basic medicines, and let you send parcels, 택배{{say:택배}}. If you can't find something, ask … 있어요?: 우산 있어요?{{say:우산 있어요?}} ("do you have umbrellas?"). For what to say if you're feeling unwell, see our pharmacy guide.

Putting it together

직원: 어서 오세요.{{say:어서 오세요}}
Clerk: Hello. (literally "welcome")

손님: 이거 1+1이에요?{{say:이거 원 플러스 원이에요?}}
Customer: Is this buy one, get one free?

직원: 네, 하나 더 가져오세요. 봉투 필요하세요?{{say:네, 하나 더 가져오세요. 봉투 필요하세요?}}
Clerk: Yes, grab another one. Do you need a bag?

손님: 아니요, 괜찮아요. 카드로 할게요.{{say:아니요, 괜찮아요. 카드로 할게요}}
Customer: No, thanks. I'll pay by card.

직원: 영수증 드릴까요?{{say:영수증 드릴까요?}}
Clerk: Would you like the receipt?

손님: 버려 주세요. 감사합니다!{{say:버려 주세요. 감사합니다}}
Customer: You can throw it away. Thank you!
