"Please" in Korean

Korean has no single word for "please". Instead, you put the politeness into the verb ending. You already know 주세요{{say:주세요}} after a noun from our restaurant guide: 물 주세요 ("water, please"). This guide shows how to ask someone to do something, and how to tell or invite someone politely.

-아/어 주세요: asking a favor

To ask someone to do something for you, take the polite present form from our verbs guide, drop the -요, and add 주세요, which comes from 주다 ("to give"). Literally, you're saying "please give me the favor of …":

VerbPresentRequestMeaning
기다리다기다려요기다려 주세요{{say:기다려 주세요}}Please wait.
말하다말해요천천히 말해 주세요{{say:천천히 말해 주세요}}Please speak slowly.
찍다찍어요사진 찍어 주세요{{say:사진 찍어 주세요}}Please take a picture (of us).
열다열어요문 열어 주세요{{say:문 열어 주세요}}Please open the door.
바꾸다바꿔요바꿔 주세요{{say:바꿔 주세요}}Please change it (for me).

You've seen this pattern already in 계산해 주세요 ("the bill, please"), from our restaurant guide, and in 여기서 세워 주세요 ("please stop here"), from our getting around guide. One common verb is written as a single word: 도와주세요{{say:도와주세요}} ("please help me"), from 도와주다.

좀: the softener

Koreans very often add 좀{{say:좀}} (literally "a little") to a request. It doesn't change the meaning, but it makes the request sound gentler, a bit like "could you …":

  • 좀 도와주세요.{{say:좀 도와주세요}} Could you help me, please?
  • 물 좀 주세요.{{say:물 좀 주세요}} Could I have some water, please?
  • 잠깐만 기다려 주세요.{{say:잠깐만 기다려 주세요}} Please wait just a moment.

-(으)세요: polite instructions and invitations

The ending -(으)세요 also means "please do", but it's used differently. With -아/어 주세요, you ask for something that helps you. With -(으)세요, you tell or invite the other person to do something, often for their own sake, as a host, a shop assistant or a teacher would:

  • 여기 앉으세요.{{say:여기 앉으세요}} Please sit here.
  • 들어오세요.{{say:들어오세요}} Please come in.
  • 맛있게 드세요.{{say:맛있게 드세요}} Enjoy your meal.
  • 왼쪽으로 가세요.{{say:왼쪽으로 가세요}} Go left.

To form it, add -세요 to a stem ending in a vowel (가다 → 가세요) and -으세요 to one ending in a consonant (앉다 → 앉으세요). If the stem ends in ㄹ, the ㄹ drops: 만들다 → 만드세요, 열다 → 여세요.

A few verbs have special respectful forms, as our politeness guide explains: 먹다 becomes 드세요, 자다 becomes 주무세요 (as in 안녕히 주무세요, "good night"), and 있다 becomes 계세요.

The same -(으)세요 form is also the polite way to talk about what someone else is doing: 어디 가세요? means "where are you going?". The situation and your tone make it clear whether it's a question, a statement or a request.

Extra polite: -아/어 주시겠어요?

With a stranger, a staff member or someone older, you can make a request even softer by turning it into a question:

  • 사진 좀 찍어 주시겠어요?{{say:사진 좀 찍어 주시겠어요?}} Would you mind taking a picture of us?
  • 다시 한번 말씀해 주시겠어요?{{say:다시 한번 말씀해 주시겠어요?}} Could you say that again, please?

-아/어 주실 수 있어요? ("could you …?") is just as polite. Our can and can't guide explains -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요.

"Please don't": -지 마세요

For a negative request, use -지 마세요, as in our guide to saying no: 걱정하지 마세요{{say:걱정하지 마세요}} ("don't worry"), 여기에 주차하지 마세요{{say:여기에 주차하지 마세요}} ("please don't park here").

With friends: -아/어 줘

Between close friends, drop the -세요 and use 줘: 이거 좀 들어 줘{{say:이거 좀 들어 줘}} ("hold this for me"), 빨리 와!{{say:빨리 와}} ("come quickly!"). Keep these for people you speak casually with, as explained in our politeness guide.

Putting it together

관광객: 저기요, 사진 좀 찍어 주시겠어요?{{say:저기요, 사진 좀 찍어 주시겠어요?}}
Tourist: Excuse me, would you mind taking a picture of us?

행인: 네, 그럼요. 조금 왼쪽으로 가세요. 좋아요!{{say:네, 그럼요. 조금 왼쪽으로 가세요. 좋아요}}
Passer-by: Sure. Move a little to the left. Great!

관광객: 한 장 더 찍어 주세요. 감사합니다!{{say:한 장 더 찍어 주세요. 감사합니다}}
Tourist: Please take one more. Thank you!
