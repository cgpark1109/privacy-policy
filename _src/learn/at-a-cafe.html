Café culture in Korea

Korea has a huge café culture: big chains and tiny independent coffee shops can be found on almost every street, and many people spend hours in cafés studying, working or meeting friends. Ordering is quick once you know a few words, and most menus are written in Hangul, which makes them good reading practice. This guide builds on our restaurant guide, so you'll recognize 주세요 and a few other phrases.

Hot or iced?

For most drinks, the first question is whether you want it hot or iced:

  • 따뜻한 걸로 주세요.{{say:따뜻한 걸로 주세요}} A hot one, please.
  • 아이스로 주세요.{{say:아이스로 주세요}} An iced one, please.

Menus often write HOT and ICE, or 따뜻한 and 아이스. Iced Americanos are so popular in Korea, even in the middle of winter, that they have a nickname: 아아{{say:아아}}, short for 아이스 아메리카노. A hot Americano is 뜨아{{say:뜨아}}, short for 뜨거운 아메리카노. These are casual slang (you'll also see 따아, from 따뜻한 아메리카노): fine with friends, and many baristas understand them, but the full name is safest when ordering.

Sizes and extras

Sizes often use borrowed words, such as 톨 (tall), 그란데 (grande) and 벤티 (venti) at some big chains, or 레귤러 (regular) and 라지 (large) at others. You can ask for a different size like this:

  • 큰 사이즈로 주세요.{{say:큰 사이즈로 주세요}} A large size, please.
  • 샷 추가해 주세요.{{say:샷 추가해 주세요}} An extra shot, please. (추가: adding)
  • 시럽은 빼 주세요.{{say:시럽은 빼 주세요}} No syrup, please. (빼다: to take out)
  • 우유를 두유로 바꿔 주세요.{{say:우유를 두유로 바꿔 주세요}} Could you change the milk to soy milk? (바꾸다: to change)
  • 얼음 적게 주세요.{{say:얼음 적게 주세요}} Less ice, please.

Notice the particle (으)로 in 아이스로 and 두유로. Here it marks your choice ("as" or "in": 아이스로, 큰 사이즈로, 따뜻한 걸로), or with 바꾸다 what something changes "into", not "to" a place as in our getting around guide.

For here or to go?

You'll almost always be asked whether you're staying:

  • 드시고 가세요?{{say:드시고 가세요?}} Will you be having it here?
  • 먹고 갈게요.{{say:먹고 갈게요}} For here, please.
  • 포장이요.{{say:포장이요}} / 가져갈게요.{{say:가져갈게요}} To go, please.

In Korea, cafés usually serve customers who are staying in mugs or glasses rather than disposable cups, so you'll be asked this before you pay. If you bring your own tumbler, 텀블러{{say:텀블러}}, many cafés give a small discount.

Paying and picking up

You usually order and pay at the counter, then wait for your drink. Many cafés use a buzzer, 진동벨{{say:진동벨}}, that vibrates when your order is ready, or call out your order number:

  • 주문하신 음료 나왔습니다.{{say:주문하신 음료 나왔습니다}} Your drink is ready.
  • 25번 고객님!{{say:이십오 번 고객님}} Customer number 25!

More and more cafés use ordering kiosks, 키오스크{{say:키오스크}}. The screens are mostly in Korean, though many have an English button. Look for 주문하기 ("order"), 매장 ("in store", i.e. for here), 포장 ("to go") and 결제 ("payment").

Staying a while

It's normal in Korea to stay in a café for a long time with one drink, to read, study or chat. Wi-Fi is widely available; to ask for the password, say 와이파이 비밀번호 뭐예요?{{say:와이파이 비밀번호 뭐예요?}} ("what's the Wi-Fi password?"). Many cafés ask you to return your tray and cup to a counter marked 반납{{say:반납}} ("return") when you leave.

Putting it together

직원: 주문하시겠어요?{{say:주문하시겠어요?}}
Barista: Are you ready to order?

손님: 아이스 아메리카노 하나하고 카페라테 하나 주세요.{{say:아이스 아메리카노 하나하고 카페라테 하나 주세요}}
Customer: One iced Americano and one café latte, please.

직원: 라테는 따뜻한 걸로 드릴까요?{{say:라테는 따뜻한 걸로 드릴까요?}}
Barista: Would you like the latte hot?

손님: 네, 따뜻한 걸로 주세요. 먹고 갈게요.{{say:네, 따뜻한 걸로 주세요. 먹고 갈게요}}
Customer: Yes, hot, please. We'll have them here.
