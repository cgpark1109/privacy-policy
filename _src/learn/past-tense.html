From present to past

Once you can make the polite present tense, the past tense is surprisingly easy: it follows almost exactly the same rules. If you haven't read it yet, start with our present tense guide, because this guide builds on it step by step.

The trick is simple: take the present tense form, and where it ends in -아요 or -어요, the past tense ends in -았어요 or -었어요.

Step 1: the 았/었 rule

Just like in the present tense, look at the last vowel of the stem:

  • If it's ㅏ or ㅗ, add 았어요.
  • If it's any other vowel, add 었어요.
VerbPresentPastMeaning
좋다좋아요좋았어요{{say:좋았어요}}It was good.
살다살아요살았어요{{say:살았어요}}I lived.
먹다먹어요먹었어요{{say:먹었어요}}I ate.
읽다읽어요읽었어요{{say:읽었어요}}I read.

Step 2: the same contractions

When the stem ends in a vowel, it merges with 았 or 었 in exactly the same way it merges in the present tense. If you know the present form, just drop 요, put ㅆ under the last syllable and add 어요: 가요 → 갔 → 갔어요.

VerbPresentPastMeaning
가다가요갔어요{{say:갔어요}}I went.
오다와요왔어요{{say:왔어요}}I came.
배우다배워요배웠어요{{say:배웠어요}}I learned.
마시다마셔요마셨어요{{say:마셨어요}}I drank.
보내다보내요보냈어요{{say:보냈어요}}I sent.

So 가요 becomes 갔어요, 와요 becomes 왔어요, and so on. The past marker ㅆ sits under the syllable as a final consonant and links to the next syllable when you say it: 갔어요 is pronounced [가써요], as our batchim guide explains.

하다 verbs: 했어요

Just as 하다 becomes 해요 in the present, it becomes 했어요{{say:했어요}} in the past:

  • 공부했어요.{{say:공부했어요}} I studied.
  • 어제 일했어요.{{say:어제 일했어요}} I worked yesterday.
  • 정말 감사했어요.{{say:정말 감사했어요}} I was really grateful.

이다 and 있다 in the past

  • 이다 becomes 였어요 after a vowel and 이었어요 after a consonant: 어제가 제 생일이었어요{{say:어제가 제 생일이었어요}} ("yesterday was my birthday"), 그 사람은 의사였어요{{say:그 사람은 의사였어요}} ("that person was a doctor").
  • 있다 becomes 있었어요 and 없다 becomes 없었어요: 시간이 없었어요{{say:시간이 없었어요}} ("I didn't have time").

Irregular verbs

The irregular verbs change their stems in the past tense the same way they do in the present:

VerbPresentPastMeaning
바쁘다바빠요바빴어요{{say:바빴어요}}I was busy.
맵다매워요매웠어요{{say:매웠어요}}It was spicy.
춥다추워요추웠어요{{say:추웠어요}}It was cold.
모르다몰라요몰랐어요{{say:몰랐어요}}I didn't know.

You met 춥다 → 추워요 in our weather guide. In the past tense it's 어제 정말 추웠어요{{say:어제 정말 추웠어요}} ("it was really cold yesterday").

Saying you didn't

As in the present, put 안 in front of the verb. With 하다 verbs made from a noun, it goes just before 했어요:

  • 안 갔어요.{{say:안 갔어요}} I didn't go.
  • 아침을 안 먹었어요.{{say:아침을 안 먹었어요}} I didn't eat breakfast.
  • 숙제 안 했어요.{{say:숙제 안 했어요}} I didn't do my homework.

To say you couldn't do something, use 못{{say:못}} instead: 못 갔어요{{say:못 갔어요}} means "I couldn't go", for example because you were sick or busy.

Time words usually come near the start of the sentence: 지난주에 부산에 갔어요{{say:지난주에 부산에 갔어요}} ("I went to Busan last week"). Note that 어제, 그저께 and 아까 don't take 에, but 지난주, 지난달 and 작년 usually do. Our days and dates guide has more time words.

Asking about the past

  • 주말에 뭐 했어요?{{say:주말에 뭐 했어요?}} What did you do on the weekend?
  • 어디에 갔어요?{{say:어디에 갔어요?}} Where did you go?
  • 재미있었어요?{{say:재미있었어요?}} Was it fun?

These questions are a great way to start a conversation on a Monday. Our questions guide has more question words.

Putting it together

가: 주말에 뭐 했어요?{{say:주말에 뭐 했어요?}}
A: What did you do on the weekend?

나: 친구하고 명동에 갔어요. 쇼핑했어요.{{say:친구하고 명동에 갔어요. 쇼핑했어요}}
B: I went to Myeong-dong with a friend. We went shopping.

가: 재미있었어요?{{say:재미있었어요?}}
A: Was it fun?

나: 네, 정말 재미있었어요. 그런데 너무 추웠어요!{{say:네, 정말 재미있었어요. 그런데 너무 추웠어요}}
B: Yes, it was really fun. But it was so cold!
