Why weather words matter

Korea has four distinct seasons, and the weather changes a lot over the year: hot, humid summers, cold, dry winters, and short, beautiful springs and autumns in between. Koreans talk about the weather often, both to make small talk and to plan their day, so a handful of weather words will come in useful almost every day. This guide uses the polite present tense from our verbs guide.

How's the weather?

The word for weather is 날씨{{say:날씨}}. To ask about it:

  • 오늘 날씨 어때요?{{say:오늘 날씨 어때요?}} How's the weather today?
  • 내일 비 와요?{{say:내일 비 와요?}} Is it going to rain tomorrow?

어때요? ("how is it?") is a handy question for almost anything: food, a movie, a new haircut.

Hot, cold and in between

KoreanMeaning
더워요{{say:더워요}}It's hot. (덥다)
추워요{{say:추워요}}It's cold. (춥다)
따뜻해요{{say:따뜻해요}}It's warm. (따뜻하다)
시원해요{{say:시원해요}}It's cool and pleasant. (시원하다)
쌀쌀해요{{say:쌀쌀해요}}It's chilly. (쌀쌀하다)
습해요{{say:습해요}}It's humid. (습하다)

덥다 and 춥다 are irregular ㅂ verbs, so they become 더워요 and 추워요, just like 맵다 becomes 매워요. 시원하다 is a lovely word: it describes a pleasant coolness, like a breeze on a hot day, but Koreans also use it for a cold drink or even a hot, spicy soup that feels refreshing.

Sun, rain and snow

KoreanMeaning
맑아요{{say:맑아요}}It's clear and sunny. (맑다)
흐려요{{say:흐려요}}It's cloudy. (흐리다)
비가 와요{{say:비가 와요}}It's raining. (literally "rain comes")
눈이 와요{{say:눈이 와요}}It's snowing.
바람이 불어요{{say:바람이 불어요}}It's windy. (literally "wind blows")

In 맑아요, the double final consonant ㄺ splits across the two syllables, so the word is pronounced [말가요], as our batchim guide explains. Don't forget an umbrella, 우산{{say:우산}}: convenience stores sell them as soon as it starts to rain.

The four seasons

SeasonKoreanWhat it's like
Spring봄{{say:봄}}Mild and sunny; cherry blossoms in early April in much of the country.
Summer여름{{say:여름}}Hot and humid, with a rainy season and sometimes typhoons.
Autumn가을{{say:가을}}Clear, dry and cool; colorful leaves in the mountains around October.
Winter겨울{{say:겨울}}Cold and dry; temperatures in Seoul often fall well below zero.

To say "in" a season, add 에: 봄에 (in spring), 겨울에 (in winter). To ask about someone's favorite season: 어느 계절을 제일 좋아해요?{{say:어느 계절을 제일 좋아해요?}} ("which season do you like best?"). Our questions guide has more question words like 어느.

Weather words special to Korea

  • 장마{{say:장마}}: the summer rainy season, usually from late June to late July, when it can rain heavily for days.
  • 태풍{{say:태풍}}: typhoon, most common in late summer and early autumn.
  • 황사{{say:황사}}: yellow dust, blown in from the deserts of inland Asia, mostly in spring.
  • 미세먼지{{say:미세먼지}}: fine dust, or air pollution. Forecasts and apps show it every day, and on bad days many people wear masks.
  • 삼한사온{{say:삼한사온}}: "three cold days, four warm days", a traditional saying about the way winter cold comes and goes.

Reading a weather forecast

A weather forecast is 일기 예보{{say:일기 예보}}. On a weather app or the news you'll see words like these:

KoreanMeaning
기온{{say:기온}}temperature
최고 / 최저{{say:최고, 최저}}high / low
영하{{say:영하}}below zero (영하 5도 is −5 °C)
강수 확률{{say:강수 확률}}chance of rain or snow
좋음 / 나쁨{{say:좋음, 나쁨}}good / bad (used for air quality)

Temperatures are in degrees Celsius, 도{{say:도}}, read with Sino-Korean numbers: 이십오 도 is 25 °C.

Weather as small talk

Just like in English, the weather is an easy way to start a conversation:

  • 오늘 진짜 덥네요.{{say:오늘 진짜 덥네요}} It's really hot today, isn't it?
  • 날씨가 좋네요.{{say:날씨가 좋네요}} Lovely weather, isn't it?

The ending -네요 adds a sense of noticing or sharing a feeling, a bit like "isn't it?" in English. It's very common in small talk.

Putting it together

가: 오늘 날씨 어때요?{{say:오늘 날씨 어때요?}}
A: How's the weather today?

나: 좀 추워요. 내일은 눈이 온대요.{{say:좀 추워요. 내일은 눈이 온대요}}
B: It's a bit cold. Tomorrow it's supposed to snow.

가: 정말요? 저는 겨울을 좋아해요!{{say:정말요? 저는 겨울을 좋아해요}}
A: Really? I love winter!
