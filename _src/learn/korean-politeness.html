Why politeness matters in Korean

In English, you can say "thanks" or "thank you very much" and the grammar stays the same. In Korean, the ending of almost every sentence changes with the person you're talking to: their age, how well you know them and the situation. "I'm going" can be 갑니다, 가요 or 가.

That might sound like a lot to learn, but in daily life you only need three styles, and one of them covers most situations. This guide shows you which one to use and how to make it.

If you're not sure, use the polite -요 style. It's never rude, it's what most learners speak with strangers, shop staff and new acquaintances, and it's the easiest to build.

Making the polite -요 form

Korean verbs and adjectives have a dictionary form that ends in -다, like 가다 (to go) or 먹다 (to eat). Take off -다 to get the stem, then look at the last vowel of the stem:

  • If it's ㅏ or ㅗ, add -아요.
  • Otherwise, add -어요.
  • Every verb ending in 하다 becomes 해요.
Dictionary formMeaningPolite form
좋다to be good좋아요{{say:좋아요}}
먹다to eat먹어요{{say:먹어요}}
있다to have, to be there있어요{{say:있어요}}
공부하다to study공부해요{{say:공부해요}}

When the stem ends in a vowel, the two vowels usually merge into one syllable, which is why the forms below look shorter:

  • 가다 → 가 + 아요 → 가요{{say:가요}} (go)
  • 오다 → 오 + 아요 → 와요{{say:와요}} (come)
  • 마시다 → 마시 + 어요 → 마셔요{{say:마셔요}} (drink)

Some common verbs are irregular, so it pays to learn the polite form along with each new verb.

"To be": -이에요 and -예요

To say "I am a student" or "this is coffee", add -이에요 after a noun that ends in a consonant and -예요 after one that ends in a vowel:

  • 학생이에요{{say:학생이에요}} (I'm a student)
  • 커피예요{{say:커피예요}} (It's coffee)

One form, many jobs

The polite form is the same for statements, questions and suggestions. Your intonation does the work: 가요. with a falling tone means "I'm going", and 가요? with a rising tone means "Are you going?". In the right context, 같이 가요{{say:같이 가요}} means "Let's go together".

The formal -습니다 form

You'll hear the formal style in announcements on the subway, in the news, in shops and at work. Add -습니다 to a stem that ends in a consonant and -ㅂ니다 to one that ends in a vowel. For questions, use -습니까? or -ㅂ니까?.

  • 먹다 → 먹습니다{{say:먹습니다}} (I eat)
  • 가다 → 갑니다{{say:갑니다}} (I go), 갑니까?{{say:갑니까}} (do you go?)
  • 하다 → 합니다{{say:합니다}} (I do)
  • Nouns take -입니다: 학생입니다{{say:학생입니다}} (I am a student)

As our batchim guide explains, ㅂ before ㄴ sounds like ㅁ, so all of these end in "-mnida". Even if you mostly speak in the polite -요 style, learn the set phrases 감사합니다 (thank you) and 죄송합니다{{say:죄송합니다}} (I'm sorry): they're the standard way to thank or apologize to someone you don't know.

Showing respect to the person: -시-

Korean has a second way to be polite: adding -시- to a verb shows respect to the person doing the action, usually someone older or senior. In the polite style, -시- plus -어요 becomes -세요, and after a stem ending in a consonant it becomes -으세요.

  • 어디 가세요?{{say:어디 가세요?}} (Where are you going?), asked politely of someone older
  • 여기 앉으세요.{{say:여기 앉으세요}} (Please sit here.)

You already know one example: 안녕하세요{{say:안녕하세요}} (hello) is 안녕하다 (to be well) with -시- and -요, literally "Are you well?". The same -세요 ending is also a polite way to ask someone to do something, as in "please sit".

Special words for respect

A few very common words have a separate respectful version. You'll hear them all the time, especially when people speak to or about their elders:

Some words work the other way: you lower yourself instead of raising the other person. In polite speech, say 저{{say:저}} instead of 나 for "I", and 저희{{say:저희}} instead of 우리 for "we" when you're talking to the people you mean to show respect to.

Calling people by name

Korean rarely uses "you" in polite speech. Instead, people use names and titles:

  • Add 씨 after an adult's name for polite but friendly address among equals: 지민 씨{{say:지민 씨}}. Never use 씨 with your own name.
  • For teachers, bosses and people in a respected role, use their title with -님 instead: 선생님{{say:선생님}} (teacher), 사장님{{say:사장님}} (company owner or boss).

Saying goodbye depends on who is leaving. Say 안녕히 가세요{{say:안녕히 가세요}} ("go in peace") to someone who is leaving, and 안녕히 계세요{{say:안녕히 계세요}} ("stay in peace") to someone who is staying while you leave.

When can I speak casually?

Switching to casual speech (반말) is a step in a relationship, so it's best not to decide it alone. The usual way is for the older or more senior person to suggest it, for example with 말 편하게 하세요{{say:말 편하게 하세요}} ("feel free to talk casually"). Between people of the same age it's often agreed openly, sometimes after asking each other's age.

Until then, stay with the polite -요 style, and add -시- when you're talking to someone clearly older. Koreans know learners are still figuring this out, and a polite effort is always appreciated.
