"It's pretty" and "a pretty bag"

In Korean, describing words like "pretty", "big" and "cold" work like verbs: 예뻐요{{say:예뻐요}} means "it's pretty" all by itself, with no separate word for "is". But when you want to put the description in front of a noun, as in "a pretty bag", the word needs a different ending. This guide shows how to make that form. It's one of the most useful things to learn after the present tense.

At the end of a sentenceBefore a noun
가방이 예뻐요. The bag is pretty.예쁜 가방{{say:예쁜 가방}} a pretty bag
방이 작아요. The room is small.작은 방{{say:작은 방}} a small room
음식이 맛있어요. The food is delicious.맛있는 음식{{say:맛있는 음식}} delicious food

-(으)ㄴ: the usual ending

Take the dictionary form, remove -다, and:

  • if the stem ends in a vowel, add ㄴ as a final consonant: 크다 → 큰, 비싸다 → 비싼, 예쁘다 → 예쁜;
  • if it ends in a consonant, add -은: 작다 → 작은, 좋다 → 좋은, 많다 → 많은.
  • 큰 집{{say:큰 집}} a big house
  • 비싼 시계{{say:비싼 시계}} an expensive watch
  • 좋은 사람{{say:좋은 사람}} a good person
  • 많은 사람들{{say:많은 사람들}} a lot of people

-는: words ending in 있다 and 없다

Describing words built on 있다 ("there is") and 없다 ("there isn't") take -는 instead:

  • 맛있다 → 맛있는 김치{{say:맛있는 김치}} delicious kimchi
  • 재미있다 → 재미있는 영화{{say:재미있는 영화}} an interesting movie
  • 멋있다 → 멋있는 사람{{say:멋있는 사람}} a cool, stylish person
  • 맛없다 → 맛없는 커피{{say:맛없는 커피}} bad-tasting coffee

Words that change their stem

Some describing words change their stem before this ending. The ㅂ and ㅎ words also change in the present tense (추워요, 빨개요), but ㄹ words change only here: 길어요 but 긴.

ㅂ becomes 우춥다, 맵다, 쉽다추운 날{{say:추운 날}} a cold day, 매운 음식{{say:매운 음식}} spicy food, 쉬운 문제{{say:쉬운 문제}} an easy question
ㄹ drops길다, 멀다긴 머리{{say:긴 머리}} long hair, 먼 나라{{say:먼 나라}} a faraway country

You've met the ㅂ change in 추워요 and 매워요 in our weather guide and taste words guide, and the ㅎ change in our colors guide.

Two descriptions: -고

To use two describing words, join them with -고 from our connecting sentences guide. Only the last one takes the noun-modifying ending:

  • 싸고 맛있는 식당{{say:싸고 맛있는 식당}} a cheap, tasty restaurant
  • 작고 귀여운 강아지{{say:작고 귀여운 강아지}} a small, cute puppy

A first look: verbs before nouns

Action verbs can describe nouns too. They use -는 for things happening now or usually (with a verb, -(으)ㄴ means the past, as in 어제 본 영화, "the movie I saw yesterday"):

  • 제가 좋아하는 음식{{say:제가 좋아하는 음식}} the food I like
  • 자주 가는 카페{{say:자주 가는 카페}} a café I often go to

Notice that Korean puts the whole description before the noun, while English uses "that" or "who" after it. This pattern takes practice; for now, it's enough to recognize it.

Putting it together

가: 이 근처에 맛있는 식당 있어요?{{say:이 근처에 맛있는 식당 있어요?}}
A: Is there a good restaurant around here?

나: 네, 싸고 맛있는 국숫집이 있어요. 조금 작은 가게예요.{{say:네, 싸고 맛있는 국숫집이 있어요. 조금 작은 가게예요}}
B: Yes, there's a cheap, tasty noodle place. It's a smallish shop.

가: 매운 음식도 있어요?{{say:매운 음식도 있어요?}}
A: Do they have spicy food too?

나: 네, 제가 좋아하는 비빔국수가 아주 매워요!{{say:네, 제가 좋아하는 비빔국수가 아주 매워요}}
B: Yes, the bibim-guksu (spicy mixed noodles) I love is really spicy!
